How to use the discount calculator

Type any two of the four boxes — original price, percent off, final price or you save — and the other two fill in. The line under the boxes says which two were used. Type a third value and it replaces the other one you typed, never the original price.

Got a coupon on top of a sale? Press Add a discount: each extra discount (a percent or an amount) comes off the price left by the one before, and the page shows the true total discount. Open Go advanced for a quantity, sales tax or VAT (on top of the price or included in it) and Compare deals: add up to three offers — a percent off, an amount off, buy X get Y free or a percent off every 2nd or 3rd item — and the cheapest is marked.

The formulas

final price = price × (1 − percent off ÷ 100) PERCENT OFF = (PRICE − FINAL PRICE) ÷ PRICE × 100

For two discounts: final price = price × (1 − first ÷ 100) × (1 − second ÷ 100). Examples, all worked out by this calculator:

Why 20% off then 15% off is not 35% off

The second discount is taken from the price that is already reduced, so it is worth less than the first. 20% off $279.00 leaves $223.20; 15% off that leaves $189.72, a total discount of 32%. The bar and the table in the calculator show every step.

Common questions

How do I calculate percent off?

Multiply the price by the percent off and divide by 100 to get the saving, then subtract it. 20% off $120.00 saves $24.00, so you pay $96.00. In the calculator, type the original price and the percent off and the rest fills in.

How do I find the discount percentage from the original and sale price?

Percent off = (original price − final price) ÷ original price × 100. A price that falls from $120.00 to $84.00 is 30% off. In the calculator, type the original price and the final price and the percent off appears.

How do I find the original price from the sale price and the percent off?

Divide the final price by (1 − percent off ÷ 100). A final price of $84.00 at 30% off means an original price of $120.00. In the calculator, type the percent off and the final price.

How do two discounts stack?

Each discount comes off the price left by the one before, so the percents do not simply add. 20% off $279.00 leaves $223.20, and a further 15% off that leaves $189.72: a total discount of 32%, not 35%. Use “Add a discount” under the boxes for any number of coupons or sales.

Is 20% off and then 10% off the same as 30% off?

No. 20% off then 10% off $100.00 leaves $72.00, a total discount of 28%. The order of two percent discounts does not change the result, but an amount off in between does.

How is sales tax added to a discounted price?

The discount comes first and the tax is charged on the lower price. $96.00 after a discount plus 8.5% tax is $8.16 of tax and $104.16 to pay. If the shelf price already includes tax, choose “Included” and the calculator shows how much of the final price is tax.

Which is better: 30% off, $25.00 off or buy 2 get 1 free?

It depends on the price and how many items you buy, so the calculator compares them. On 3 items at $100.00 each: 30% off costs $210.00, $25.00 off costs $275.00 and buy 2 get 1 free costs $200.00, so the free item wins here. Open Go advanced, add the offers under “Compare deals” and the best one is marked.

What does 50% off every 2nd item mean?

The 2nd, 4th, 6th … item is half price and the others are full price. For 2 items at $100.00 you pay $150.00, a saving of 25% overall. “Every 3rd item” works the same way with items 3, 6, 9 ….

Can a discount be more than 100%?

No. 100% off is free, so the calculator accepts percents from 0 to 100, and an amount off can never be more than the price left.

Does the currency selector convert my numbers?

No. It only changes the symbol and the decimals shown. Type your numbers in the currency you use.
