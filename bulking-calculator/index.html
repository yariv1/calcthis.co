How to use the bulking calculator

Pick male or female, enter your age, height (centimetres, inches or 5'10, metres), activity level and weight — or choose I know it and type your maintenance calories. Then set the surplus the way you think about it: a pace in percent of body weight a week, a percent above maintenance, or kcal a day — and see all three. Add a goal weight for the time to goal.

Open Go advanced for training experience, body fat (Katch-McArdle), your own protein and fat per kg and the number of meals.

The calorie surplus formula

Calories to bulk = maintenance + surplus · maintenance = BMR × activity factor PACE: SURPLUS = % × WEIGHT (KG) × 7,700 ÷ 7 · PERCENT: SURPLUS = % × MAINTENANCE

For a man of 80 kg and 180 cm, 28 years old, moderate activity: BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor) = 1,790 kcal, maintenance = 1,790 × 1.55 = 2,775 kcal. A pace of 0.25% a week is 0.20 kg, a surplus of 220 kcal a day, so about 2,995 kcal.

Bigger is not better

In an 8-week trial of 17 trained lifters, larger surpluses mainly added fat rather than muscle thickness or squat strength (Helms 2023, small and short). That is why the calculator starts at the low end, flags anything above the review’s ranges and offers no “dirty bulk” preset. See also the calorie deficit calculator for the other direction.

Common questions

How many calories should I eat to bulk?

Eat your maintenance calories plus a surplus. For a man of 80 kg and 180 cm, 28 years old, with moderate activity, maintenance is about 2,775 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor 1,790 × 1.55). A surplus of 10–20% above maintenance, the range in a 2019 review of off-season bodybuilding nutrition, is 277–555 kcal, so about 3,052–3,329 kcal a day. The calculator does this for your numbers.

How do I calculate a calorie surplus?

A calorie surplus is what you eat above maintenance. Step 1: maintenance = BMR × activity factor. Step 2: add the surplus. You can set the surplus as a pace — 0.25% of your body weight a week is 0.20 kg for 80 kg, which at 7,700 kcal per kg is 220 kcal a day — or as a percent of maintenance, or as kcal. Target = 2,775 + 220 = 2,995 kcal.

How big should a bulking calorie surplus be?

The 2019 review gives about 10–20% above maintenance and a target gain of 0.25–0.5% of body weight a week for novice and intermediate lifters, with advanced lifters more conservative. For the 80 kg man that is 220–440 kcal a day (7.9–15.9% of maintenance). In an 8-week trial of 17 trained lifters, larger surpluses mainly added fat rather than muscle (Helms 2023), so more is not better. The calculator flags a surplus above these ranges.

How much weight should I gain per week when bulking?

About 0.25–0.5% of your body weight a week, from the review: 0.20–0.40 kg (0.44–0.88 lb) a week at 80 kg. The surplus for a given weight is simple: about 2.75 kcal per kg of body weight a day for 0.25% a week and 5.50 for 0.5%. This is scale weight; the calculator does not say how much is muscle, because no source I read gives a reliable split.

How many extra calories add 1 lb or 1 kg?

About 3,500 kcal per lb or 7,700 kcal per kg of body weight is the usual rule of thumb, used here as the simple estimate. It is an approximation: the real mix of muscle, fat and water changes with training, diet and the person, and maintenance rises as you gain, which is why the calculator also shows a more realistic curve.

How much protein, fat and carbs do I need when bulking?

The 2019 review gives protein of 1.6–2.2 g/kg a day (0.40–0.55 g/kg per meal over 3–6 meals), fat of 0.5–1.5 g/kg a day and carbohydrate of at least 3–5 g/kg a day to fuel training. The calculator starts at 1.8 g/kg protein and 1.0 g/kg fat and gives carbohydrate the rest: for the 80 kg man at 2,995 kcal that is 144 g protein, 80 g fat and 425 g carbohydrate, and it warns when a figure leaves the review’s range.

Why does the calculator show two times to reach my goal weight?

The simple rule assumes you keep gaining at the same pace by raising calories as you go. In reality maintenance rises by about 16 kcal for every kg you gain, so eating the same amount produces a smaller surplus each week. For the example (80 kg to 85 kg at 220 kcal of surplus) the simple rule gives about 25 weeks and the realistic one about 31 weeks. The ladder under the chart shows what to eat each few weeks to keep the pace.

What is a lean bulk, and what is a dirty bulk?

A lean bulk is a small, controlled surplus, at the low end of the ranges above, so weight rises slowly. A “dirty bulk” means eating as much as possible. The only trial I read comparing surpluses (Helms 2023, small and short) found that larger surpluses mainly added fat, so the calculator defaults to the low end and has no dirty-bulk preset.

Can women use this bulking calculator?

Yes. Maintenance uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for either sex, and the surplus is set as a percent of body weight or maintenance, so it scales with the person. The review’s ranges are not given separately for women, so the same ranges are used. Choose female or male and enter your own numbers.

Should I bulk or lose fat first?

The calculator does not decide that. A bulk adds weight; if you want to lose fat first or at the same time, use the calorie deficit calculator, and see the protein intake calculator for protein on its own.
