How to use the semester grade calculator

Pick a layout (quarters + final, midterm + final or three terms) or add your own terms. For each one enter the grade — a percentage, or a letter if you switch to Letters — and its weight. Leave the grade blank for any term you have not had yet.

The big number is your grade so far. Below it you get the lowest and highest the semester can end on, the score you need on the rest for every letter, and a chart that shows your semester grade at every possible score. Type a target grade, or tap a letter, to focus the answer on it.

The semester grade formula

Semester grade = Σ (grade × weight) ÷ Σ weights EACH TERM'S GRADE TIMES ITS SHARE OF THE SEMESTER

Two quarters of 78% and 85%, each worth 40%, with the final (20%) still to come: (78 × 40 + 85 × 40) ÷ 80 = 81.5% so far. If the final is 80%, the semester grade is (78 × 40 + 85 × 40 + 80 × 20) ÷ 100 = 81.2%, a B.

What you need on the final

To finish on a target grade T, the score needed on the remaining term is (T × total weight − points so far) ÷ remaining weight. For the example:

GradeSemester needsNeeded on the final
A90%Out of reach
B80%74%
C70%24%
D60%Secured

A is out of reach here: even 100% on the final tops out at 85.2%. The calculator shows this table, and the chart behind it, for your own numbers.

Percent or letters?

Percentages give exact results. If you only have letters, the calculator converts each one to the middle of its band in the plus/minus scale (A+ 98.5%, A 94.5%, A- 91%, B+ 88%, and so on down to F at 29.5%). Two equally weighted terms of B+ and A- come to 89.5%.

Empty terms, extra credit and dropped scores

  • Empty terms. By default a blank term is "not graded yet" and left out of your grade so far. Choose Zero to see the grade if it stays empty.
  • Extra credit. Added to the semester grade after the weighted average, with an optional cap.
  • Drop the lowest. Type a term's scores as a list (88, 92, 75) and drop the lowest one or more: 85% becomes 90% with one dropped.
  • Highest score. If a term can go above 100%, raise this so the needed scores and the chart use the right ceiling.

For a course split into homework, quizzes and exams use the grade calculator; for one exam, the final grade calculator; for your GPA across courses, the GPA calculator.

Common questions

How do I calculate my semester grade?

Multiply each term's grade by its weight, add the results and divide by the total weight. With a quarter 1 grade of 78% and a quarter 2 grade of 85% (each worth 40%) and a final exam of 80% (worth 20%), the semester grade is (78 × 40 + 85 × 40 + 80 × 20) ÷ 100 = 81.2%, a B.

What do I need on the final exam to get an A?

It depends on your grades so far and the final's weight. With 78% and 85% in two quarters worth 40% each, an A (90%) would need 124% on a final worth 20% — out of reach, since even 100% leaves you at 85.2%. A B (80%) needs 74%, a C (70%) needs 24%, and a D (60%) is already secured. The calculator shows every letter at once.

How do I find my semester grade if I only have some terms graded?

Leave the unfinished terms blank. The calculator shows your grade so far (81.5% in the example), the lowest and highest the semester can end on (65.2% to 85.2%), and what you need on the rest. Under Go advanced you can choose to count empty terms as zero instead.

Do the weights have to add up to 100?

No. The calculator scales whatever you enter to 100%, so weights of 2, 2 and 1 work the same as 40, 40 and 20. It tells you when your weights do not add up to 100 so you can check them.

Can I use letter grades?

Yes — switch “Grades as” to Letters. A letter counts as the middle of its band in the plus/minus table (for example B+ is 88% and A- is 91%, F is 29.5%), so a B+ and an A- in two equally weighted terms give 89.5%. Type percentages for exact results.

How do I drop the lowest score?

Under Go advanced, set “Drop the lowest” to 1 (or more) and type a term's scores as a list such as 88, 92, 75. The calculator drops the lowest score and averages the rest: 85% without a drop, 90% with one.

What if I have extra credit?

Under Go advanced, enter the extra-credit percentage points and an optional cap. They are added to the semester grade after the weighted average, so 2 points of extra credit turns an 81.2% into 83.2%.

How is this different from the final grade calculator?

The final grade calculator answers one question — what score do I need on one exam. This page works with the whole semester: any number of terms, the grade so far, the best and worst the semester can end on and the score you need for every letter at once. For one exam, use the final grade calculator; for a course split into homework, quizzes and tests, the grade calculator.
