Common questions

What units can this unit converter convert?

Length (inches, cm, feet, meters, miles, km), weight (kg, pounds, ounces, grams, stone, tons), volume (liters, gallons, cups, mL, fluid ounces — US and imperial), temperature (Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin, Rankine), area (square feet, square meters, acres, hectares), speed (mph, km/h, m/s, knots), data storage (bytes, KB, MB, GB, TB and the binary KiB, MiB, GiB), cooking measures (cups to grams per ingredient, oven temperatures) and CSS units (px, rem, em, pt, %).

How do I convert units?

Pick the converter, type the value, choose the unit you have and the unit you want. Every conversion is one multiplication by a fixed factor — for example 1 in = 2.54 cm and 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg — except temperature, which also adds an offset: 212°F = 100°C.

What is the difference between metric and imperial units?

Metric units (meter, gram, liter) are based on powers of ten; imperial and US customary units (inch, pound, gallon) are not. The two are linked by exact definitions: 1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg and 1 gal = 3.785411784 L. A US gallon and an imperial gallon differ: 3.7854118 L against 4.54609 L.

Are the conversions exact?

The factors are exact international definitions (BIPM, NIST and IEC), and the result is shown to 8 significant digits by default — choose up to 10 significant digits, or a fixed number of decimals, under Go advanced. The one approximate case is cooking: cups to grams uses typical ingredient densities (about ±5%).

Why do the converters have separate pages?

Each converter does one job: the length converter shows a ruler, the temperature converter a thermometer scale, the px to rem converter a live text sample, and the cooking converter knows what a cup of flour weighs. Use the converter menu at the top of the calculator to switch.
