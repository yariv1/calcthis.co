How to use the weight loss percentage calculator

Pick your weight units (kg, lb, stones, or stones and pounds), then type your starting weight and your current weight. Know different numbers? Change I know my to starting weight and percent, current weight and percent, or any other pair, and the calculator finds the rest.

Add a goal weight to see how far along you are, or open Go advanced and add the date you started for your average loss per week.

The weight loss percentage formula

Percent lost = (starting weight − current weight) ÷ starting weight × 100 WEIGHT LOST = STARTING WEIGHT − CURRENT WEIGHT

From 200 lb to 180 lb: 200 − 180 = 20 lb lost, and 20 ÷ 200 × 100 = 10.0%. From 90 kg to 84 kg: 6 ÷ 90 × 100 = 6.7%. The unit cancels, so kilograms, pounds and stones all give the same percent.

What counts as a good percentage?

  • 3% to 5%. NHLBI reports that a loss of 3% to 5% of current weight can lower triglycerides and blood glucose.
  • 5% to 10% over about 6 months. What health professionals recommend for people with overweight or obesity, per NHLBI. The CDC’s example: 5% of 200 pounds is 10 pounds.
  • 1 to 2 pounds a week. The gradual pace the CDC links with keeping weight off.

Percent of body weight or percent of goal?

Percent of body weight compares you with where you started, so it is fair across different body sizes. Percent of your goal compares you with the weight you planned to lose: from 200 lb with a goal of 160 lb, losing 20 lb is 10% of your body weight but 50% of the way. With a goal weight entered, the calculator shows both. To plan calories or a date, use the weight loss calculator by date.

Common questions

How do I calculate weight loss percentage?

Subtract your current weight from your starting weight, divide by the starting weight and multiply by 100. For 200 lb to 180 lb: (200 − 180) ÷ 200 × 100 = 10.0%. For 90 kg to 84 kg: 6 ÷ 90 × 100 = 6.7%. The unit cancels, so it is the same in kg, lb or stones.

What is a good weight loss percentage?

For people with overweight or obesity, NHLBI says health professionals recommend losing 5% to 10% of initial weight over about 6 months, and that a loss of 3% to 5% can lower triglycerides and blood glucose; losing more than that can improve blood pressure and cholesterol. The CDC gives an example: 5% of 200 pounds is 10 pounds. Whether a percentage is right for you depends on your health, so ask your clinician.

How much is 5%, 10% or 20% of my body weight?

Multiply your weight by the percent: 5% of 200 lb is 10 lb (the CDC’s example), 10% is 20 lb and 20% is 40 lb. The calculator lists 3%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% for your own starting weight, and a click on a row sets it as your goal.

How do I find my weight after losing a percentage?

Starting weight × (1 − percent ÷ 100). For 90 kg and a 5% loss: 90 × 0.95 = 85.5 kg, which is 4.5 kg lost. In the calculator choose “Starting weight and percent lost”.

How do I find my starting weight from my current weight and a percentage?

Current weight ÷ (1 − percent ÷ 100). If you now weigh 180 lb after losing 10%: 180 ÷ 0.9 = 200 lb. In the calculator choose “Current weight and percent lost”.

Why divide by the starting weight, not the current weight?

The percentage tells you how much of where you began you have lost, so the starting weight is the base. 200 lb to 180 lb is 20 ÷ 200 = 10% lost. Dividing by 180 would give 11.1%, which is the percentage you would have to gain back to return to 200 lb — a different question.

What is the difference between percent of body weight and percent of my goal?

Percent of body weight is how much of your starting weight you lost. Percent of your goal is how much of the weight you planned to lose you have lost: starting at 200 lb with a goal of 160 lb, losing 20 lb is 10% of your body weight but 50% of the way to your goal. Add a goal weight and the calculator shows both.

How fast is it safe to lose weight?

The CDC says people who lose weight at a gradual, steady pace — about 1 to 2 pounds (0.45–0.9 kg) a week — are more likely to keep it off. Under Go advanced, enter the date you started and the calculator shows your average loss per week and as a percent of your starting weight.

Is this the same as a percentage change calculator?

The arithmetic is the same percent decrease, but this page is built for body weight: it takes pounds, kilograms and stones, solves from any two values and shows progress to a goal. For any other before-and-after number use the percentage change calculator.

How do I plan the calories or the date for a percentage?

Use the weight loss calculator by date: it turns a goal weight and a date into calories a day and shows the fastest safe date. The calorie deficit calculator starts from a deficit instead.

How do I enter stones and pounds?

Choose “st + lb” and type a weight like 12 st 5 lb (or 12 5, or 12st5). A single number is read as stones.
