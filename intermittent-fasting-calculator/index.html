How to use the intermittent fasting calculator

Choose a method, then tell the calculator the one time you know: your last bite of the day, your first meal, or your bedtime and how long before it you want to stop eating. The result is your eating window, a 24-hour clock with your meals, where you are right now, and where your fast ends against the research range for the metabolic switch.

For 5:2 pick your two low-calorie days; for alternate-day see the 14-day pattern; for a 24–72 hour fast see when it ends. Go advanced adds a different weekend window, meals, a ramp-up plan and calories from your own details.

The fasting window maths

first meal = last bite + fasting hours · eating window = 24 − fasting hours 16:8 = 16 HOURS FASTING, 8 HOURS EATING

Last bite at 8:00 PM on 16:8: 8:00 PM + 16 h = 12:00 PM, so you eat 12:00 PM – 8:00 PM (8 hours). Know the first meal instead — 12:00 PM — and the last bite is 12:00 PM + 8 h = 8:00 PM. On 18:6 with the same last bite the first meal is 2:00 PM.

Does the eating window help you lose weight?

The two long trials found that a window on its own gave no significant extra weight loss. In the 12-week TREAT trial (Lowe 2020, 116 adults) 16:8 from 12 pm to 8 pm lost −0.94 kg against −0.68 kg for three structured meals — a difference of −0.26 kg (P = .63). In a 12-month trial (Liu 2022, 139 adults with obesity) an 8 a.m.–4 p.m. window plus calorie restriction lost −8.0 kg against −6.3 kg for calorie restriction alone, a difference of −1.8 kg (95% CI −4.0 to 0.4; P = 0.11). Total calories still decide; a window helps if it makes eating less easier for you. Work out your calories with the calorie deficit calculator.

The metabolic switch

The review by Anton and colleagues (2018) describes the metabolic switch as the shift from glucose to fatty acids and ketones when liver glycogen is used up, and says it typically occurs between 12 and 36 hours after the last food — earlier or later depending on how much glycogen you start with and how much you exercise. A 16-hour daily fast therefore ends 4 hours past the 12-hour mark; most people’s daily windows never reach 36 hours.

5:2 and alternate-day fasting

The 5:2 diet is normal eating five days and no more than 500 calories on two days. Alternate-day modified fasting uses less than 25% of baseline energy on the low days. If you maintain your weight on 2,400 kcal, 5:2 averages 1857 kcal a day (a weekly gap of 3,800 kcal, about 0.49 kg) and alternate-day averages 1500 kcal if the normal days are eaten at maintenance.

  • Anton SD, Moehl K, Donahoo WT et al., Flipping the Metabolic Switch: Understanding and Applying the Health Benefits of Fasting, Obesity 2018;26:254–268 — the metabolic switch “typically occurs between 12 to 36 hours after cessation of food consumption depending on the liver glycogen content at the beginning of the fast” and exercise; time-restricted feeding typically restricts eating to 8–12 hours a day; the 5:2 diet is no more than 500 calories on two days a week; alternate-day modified fasting uses less than 25% of baseline energy on low days (read November 2026).
  • Lowe DA et al., Effects of Time-Restricted Eating on Weight Loss and Other Metabolic Parameters in Women and Men With Overweight and Obesity: The TREAT Randomized Clinical Trial, JAMA Internal Medicine 2020;180:1491 — 116 adults, 12 weeks of 16:8 (12 pm–8 pm) against three structured meals: −0.94 kg against −0.68 kg, no significant difference between groups (−0.26 kg, P = .63).
  • Liu D et al., Calorie Restriction with or without Time-Restricted Eating in Weight Loss, New England Journal of Medicine 2022;386:1495–1504 — 139 adults with obesity, 12 months, eating 8 a.m.–4 p.m. plus calorie restriction against calorie restriction alone: −8.0 kg against −6.3 kg, difference −1.8 kg (95% CI −4.0 to 0.4; P = 0.11).

Common questions

How do I calculate my intermittent fasting window?

Your fasting window starts at your last bite and lasts the hours of your method; your eating window is the rest of the 24 hours. On 16:8 with the last bite at 8:00 PM you fast 16 hours, so you eat again at 12:00 PM and the eating window is 12:00 PM – 8:00 PM. Choose First meal instead if that is the time you know: a first meal at 12:00 PM on 16:8 means the last bite is at 8:00 PM.

What are the common intermittent fasting schedules?

The daily ones are 12:12, 14:10, 16:8, 18:6, 20:4 and OMAD (one meal a day, 23:1) — the first number is hours fasting, the second hours eating. 5:2 is normal eating five days a week and no more than 500 calories on two days; alternate-day fasting alternates low days (less than 25% of your usual calories) with normal days. A review of the research (Anton 2018) describes daily time-restricted eating as typically 8–12 hours of eating.

Does intermittent fasting help you lose weight?

Mostly through eating fewer calories. In a 12-week trial of 116 adults, 16:8 eating (12 pm–8 pm) lost no more weight than three structured meals — −0.94 kg against −0.68 kg, a difference of −0.26 kg that was not significant (Lowe 2020). In a 12-month trial of 139 adults with obesity, adding an 8 a.m.–4 p.m. window to calorie restriction gave −8.0 kg against −6.3 kg, also not a significant difference (−1.8 kg, P = 0.11; Liu 2022). A window can make eating less easier for some people; it is not a shortcut by itself.

What is the metabolic switch?

It is the point in a fast when the liver’s glycogen runs low and the body shifts toward fat and ketones for fuel. A review of the research (Anton 2018) says it typically occurs between 12 and 36 hours after the last food, depending on the liver glycogen you start with and how much you exercise. The calculator shows where your fast ends against that 12–36 hour range — on 16:8 it ends 4 hours past the 12-hour point.

What is the 5:2 diet?

You eat normally five days a week and eat no more than 500 calories on two days (Anton 2018). Pick the two low days in the calculator. With details added under Go advanced it shows your average calories a day and the weekly calorie gap: for someone who maintains weight on 2,400 kcal, 5:2 averages about 1857 kcal a day if the other days are eaten at maintenance — about 0.49 kg a week.

What is alternate-day fasting?

Every other day is a low day with less than 25% of your usual energy (alternate-day modified fasting, Anton 2018) and the other days are eaten normally. For someone who maintains weight on 2,400 kcal a low day is up to 600 kcal. The calculator shows the 14-day pattern.

How do I work up to 16:8 or a longer fast?

Use Go advanced: enter the hours you fast now and the weeks you want to take. The ramp table moves the window in half-hour steps. From a 12-hour fast to 16 hours over 4 weeks with the last bite fixed at 8:00 PM, the first meal moves from 8:00 AM to 9:00 AM, 10:00 AM, 11:00 AM and 12:00 PM.

What if my weekends are different?

Under Go advanced choose “Different at weekends” and enter the weekend time; the result shows both windows, and the live status uses the weekend window on Saturday and Sunday.

Can I build my window around when I sleep?

Yes. Choose “My day”, enter your bedtime and how many hours before bed you want to stop eating (3 if you leave it empty). With a bedtime of 11:00 PM and a 3-hour gap on 16:8, the last bite is at 8:00 PM and the first meal at 12:00 PM.

Who should not try intermittent fasting?

Intermittent fasting is not suitable for everyone. Talk to a doctor first if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, have had an eating disorder, have diabetes or take medication that has to be taken with food, are underweight, or are considering a fast of 24 hours or more. The calculator asks about these and does not show a schedule for the first three.
