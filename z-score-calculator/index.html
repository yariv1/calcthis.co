    How to use the z-score calculator

    Type your score, the mean of the group and its standard deviation: the z-score, the percentile and the areas under the curve appear at once, with a bell curve that shows your score on both the z scale and your own scale. Already know the z-score or the percentile? Type it in its box together with two of the three boxes above, and the missing one (score, mean or standard deviation) is worked out.

    Choose Compare two to see which of two results is better compared with its own group, even when they come from different tests. Go advanced for the z-score of a sample mean, the critical z-score for a confidence level, the share of the group between two z-scores, and a z-score for every number in a pasted list.

    The formula

    z = (x − μ) ÷ σ SAMPLE MEAN OF n VALUES: z = (x̄ − μ) ÷ (σ ÷ √n) · SCORE FROM z: x = μ + z × σ

    x is the value, μ the mean and σ the standard deviation. A positive z-score is above the mean, a negative one below it, and 0 is the mean itself. Worked out with this calculator: x = 85, μ = 75, σ = 10 gives z = 1.0000; the area to its left is 0.8413, so 84.13% of the group scores lower and 15.87% higher.

    How much of a group lies near the mean?

    For a bell-shaped group the share within a given distance of the mean is fixed:

    Distance from the meanShare of the group
    within 1 standard deviation68.27%
    within 2 standard deviations95.45%
    within 3 standard deviations99.73%

    So a z-score beyond 2 is further out than 95.45% of the group, and beyond 3 further out than 99.73%.

    Compare scores from different tests

    Raw scores from different scales cannot be compared directly. Turn each into a z-score first: 85 on a test with mean 75 and standard deviation 10 has z = 1.00, while 640 on a test with mean 500 and standard deviation 100 has z = 1.40. The second is higher than 91.92% of its group, the first than 84.13% of its group, so the second is the better result.

    Z-score of a sample mean

    When the score is the average of n values, divide the standard deviation by √n first. A sample of 36 with a mean of 76, a population mean of 75 and σ = 10 has a standard error of 1.6667 and z = 0.60, higher than 72.57% of sample means.

    Common questions

    How do I calculate a z-score?

    Subtract the mean from your score and divide by the standard deviation: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. For a score of 85 with a mean of 75 and a standard deviation of 10, z = (85 − 75) ÷ 10 = 1.00.

    What does a z-score tell me?

    How many standard deviations a value is above (positive) or below (negative) the mean. A z-score of 0 is exactly the mean. A z-score of 1.00 means one standard deviation above it, which is higher than 84.13% of a normal group.

    How do I turn a z-score into a percentile?

    The percentile is the area under the standard normal curve to the left of z. Type the z-score in the calculator and read “Below z” in the areas table: z = 1.00 gives 84.13%, so about 15.87% score higher. The z table on this page lists the same areas.

    How do I find the z-score for a percentile?

    Type the percentile in the Percentile box. The 95th percentile has z = 1.6449, the 50th has z = 0 and the 5th has z = −1.6449. The percentile table on this page lists more.

    What z-score do I need for 90%, 95% or 99% confidence?

    The two-tailed critical z-scores are ±1.6449 for 90%, ±1.9600 for 95% and ±2.5758 for 99%. In Go advanced, type a confidence level to get its critical z-score; the table on this page lists more levels, one-tailed too.

    How do I compare scores from different tests?

    Switch to “Compare two” and type each score with its own mean and standard deviation. A 85 on a test with mean 75 and standard deviation 10 has z = 1.00; a 640 on a test with mean 500 and standard deviation 100 has z = 1.40. The second is the better result compared with its group (91.92% vs 84.13%).

    What is the z-score of a sample mean?

    Go advanced and type the sample size n: the standard error σ ÷ √n replaces σ. A sample of 36 people with a mean of 76, when the population mean is 75 and σ = 10, gives z = (76 − 75) ÷ (10 ÷ √36) = 0.60.

    How do I find the score, mean or standard deviation from a z-score?

    Type the z-score (or a percentile) and any two of the score, mean and standard deviation: the empty box is worked out. For example z = 1.00, mean 75 and standard deviation 10 give a score of 85 (x = μ + z × σ).

    Is a z-score above 2 or 3 unusual?

    For a bell-shaped group, 4.55% of values lie more than 2 standard deviations from the mean (either side) and 0.27% lie more than 3 away. The calculator shows the exact share outside your own z-score, so you can judge for yourself.

    Can I get a z-score for every number in a list?

    Yes. Go advanced, paste the numbers separated by commas, spaces or new lines, and a table shows each value’s z-score and marks those beyond ±2 and ±3. Choose population (÷ n) or sample (÷ n − 1) for the standard deviation, and use the mean and spread above to score a new value.

    What is the difference between a z-score and a p-value?

    A z-score says how far a value is from the mean in standard deviations. A p-value is the probability of a result at least that extreme. The areas table turns your z-score into one-tailed and two-tailed probabilities; for tests of a hypothesis use the p-value calculator.
