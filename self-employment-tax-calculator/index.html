    How to use the self-employment tax calculator

    Type what your clients paid you and your business expenses. The result updates as you type: self-employment tax, federal income tax, what you keep and the four estimated payments. Pick a state to add its tax. Paid in another currency? Choose it at the top and type its value in US dollars: the tax is worked out in dollars and shown in both. If you know your payments by month, choose Add it up month by month: the months fill the yearly total, empty months can use your average, and each month shows what to set aside. To work backwards, choose Income I need and type the take-home pay you want.

    Under Go advanced add mileage and a home office, a W-2 job, health insurance, retirement contributions, itemized deductions, children, and last year’s tax for the penalty-free amount.

    The 2026 self-employment tax formula

    SE tax = profit × 92.35% × 15.3% 12.4% Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages and earnings combined · 2.9% Medicare has no limit · extra 0.9% Medicare above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly)

    Deductions for the self-employed

    Business expenses come off before both taxes. Driving counts at 72.5¢ a mile from January to June 2026 and 76¢ from July; a home office can use $5 a square foot for up to 300. Half of the self-employment tax, health insurance premiums and retirement contributions come off income before income tax.

    The qualified business income deduction removes up to 20% of business profit from taxable income. It starts to shrink above $201,750 of taxable income ($403,500 married filing jointly) for a business without employees or property, and from 2026 it is at least $400 when qualified business income is $1,000 or more.

    Retirement limits for 2026

    A SEP-IRA takes up to 20% of profit after half the self-employment tax, to $72,000. A solo 401(k) adds a $24,500 deferral of your own (plus $8,000 from age 50, or $11,250 at 60 to 63). A traditional IRA is limited to $7,500, or $8,600 from age 50, and may not be fully deductible if you also have a retirement plan.

    Quarterly estimated payments

    Nobody withholds tax from 1099 income, so you usually pay it in four parts: April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. If your wages already withhold enough, you may owe nothing extra.

    Common questions

    What is self-employment tax?

    It is Social Security and Medicare for people who work for themselves: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare, 15.3% together, on 92.35% of net profit. Income tax comes on top of it.

    How is self-employment tax calculated?

    Take your profit, multiply by 92.35%, then apply 15.3%. On $80,000 of profit that is $73,880.00 of net earnings and $11,303.64 of tax.

    Do I pay it on small amounts?

    Self-employment tax applies when net earnings are $400 or more. This is true whether or not a client sends you a form.

    How much should I set aside from each payment?

    It depends on your income, filing status and state. For a single person with $80,000 of profit and federal tax only, the business adds $16,647.47 of tax, 20.8% of every payment. The “A payment just arrived” box works it out for one payment at your next-dollar rate.

    When are 2026 estimated taxes due?

    April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. The January payment is not needed if you file and pay in full by February 1, 2027.

    How much must I pay to avoid a penalty?

    Generally the smaller of 90% of your 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax, counting withholding. The 100% becomes 110% if your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately). No payments are needed if you expect to owe under $1,000.

    What if I also have a W-2 job?

    Your wages use up the $184,500 Social Security limit first. With $160,000 of wages and $40,000 of profit, only $24,500.00 of self-employment earnings pay the 12.4% part.

    Do business expenses lower the tax?

    Yes, both self-employment and income tax. In the $80,000 example an extra $1,000 of expense saves about $230.51.

    Does a SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) lower self-employment tax?

    No. It lowers income tax. Putting $10,000 into a SEP-IRA in the example cuts federal tax from $16,647.47 to $15,687.47, while self-employment tax stays $11,303.64.

    Can I deduct half of self-employment tax?

    Yes. Half of it comes off your income before income tax, $5,651.82 in the example. It does not reduce the self-employment tax itself.

    How much do I need to bill to take home a certain amount?

    Choose “Income I need”. To keep $60,000 a year with federal tax only, a single person needs about $75,643.16 of profit.

    What should I bill to match a salaried job?

    Type the salary under “Weighing a job offer?”. With federal tax only, a single person needs about $102,221.94 of billing to match a $100,000 salary before benefits.

    What if my clients pay me in another currency?

    Choose the currency at the top of the calculator and type how many US dollars one unit is worth. Your amounts are converted at that rate, the US tax is worked out in dollars, and the quarterly payments are shown in dollars. The page does not look up exchange rates.

    Which states are included?

    Thirty-eight states are estimated from their paycheck withholding formulas. For Connecticut, Delaware, the District of Columbia, Idaho, Kansas, Maine, Mississippi, New Mexico, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Vermont and West Virginia you type your effective rate.
