## Parity list (every input / output; each matched or beaten; a drop needs his quoted sign-off)
Inputs: initial amount; rate (annual); compounding frequency (annual ... daily, continuous; 360 vs 365 day year); term in years and months (and days for the daily variant); regular deposit amount + frequency (weekly ... yearly); deposits at beginning or end of period; withdrawals instead of deposits; tax rate on interest; inflation rate; currency; rate range (low / high scenarios); solve for rate, time, principal, total (CalculatorSoup); APY <-> APR conversion (calculator.net).

## Differentiators (none of the above has them)
**Visual:** one layered growth chart: initial money, your own deposits and the interest on top, with a marker on the year the yearly interest passes the yearly deposits, plus a dashed "without compounding" line (simple interest) so the gap compounding adds is visible. Competitors show a pie or a plain stacked bar.
**Functional (main):** a goal solver in the same boxes: type the amount you want and leave one thing empty — final balance, monthly deposit needed, years needed, or the rate needed — and it is solved (CalculatorSoup solves some, only one at a time; none solves the monthly deposit, the most common question). Plus: low / base / high rate scenarios (Investor.gov parity, as three lines), deposits that grow by a yearly percentage (raises), a withdrawal phase after the saving years, after-tax and inflation-adjusted values (calculator.net parity), and the same result for every compounding frequency side by side with the extra the faster frequency earns.
Default view stays minimal: amount, rate, years, monthly deposit, compounding; everything else under Go advanced. Currency picker always visible top-right of the first card (his hard rule 2026-11-13), copied from the markup page. Labels in his wording, empty start, every field visibly reacts, optional fields never hide the result.
