How to use the wallpaper calculator

Pick your roll — a US double roll, a Euro roll, or type in the width and length printed on yours — then enter the room's length, width and ceiling height. If your paper has a pattern, add the pattern repeat from the label. The walls are drawn as you type, each strip coloured by the roll it's cut from, and the result shows how many rolls to buy, what's left over, and what it costs. Papering several rooms? Add each one and the project total combines them into one order.

Why wallpaper isn't counted by area

Paint is spread over an area; wallpaper is hung in whole vertical strips. Each strip is cut to the wall height plus a trim allowance, and the offcut from the bottom of a roll is usually too short to use. Dividing wall area by roll area ignores that, so it quietly undercounts. This calculator counts strips the way a paperhanger does, then shows the plain area answer next to the real one so you can see the difference.

Strips = wall width ÷ roll width · Rolls = strips ÷ strips per roll CUT LENGTH = WALL HEIGHT + TRIM, ROUNDED UP TO THE PATTERN REPEAT

Worked example: a 12 × 14 room

A 12 × 14 ft room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows, papered with a 20.5 in × 33 ft double roll and no pattern repeat:

The walls take 8 + 9 + 8 + 9 = 34 strips. Each strip is 96 in + 4 in trim = 100 in, so a 396 in roll gives 3 strips. 34 ÷ 3 = 12 rolls, leaving about 96 in of offcuts per roll. A plain area estimate (subtracting the door and windows) would have said 7.

Pattern repeat and match type

The pattern repeat is the vertical distance before the design repeats. To line strips up, each one is cut to a whole number of repeats, so a bigger repeat wastes more paper. With a straight match every strip is the same length. With a half-drop, alternate strips are offset by half a repeat, so every other strip needs one extra repeat — the roll count rises noticeably. A random match (textures, grasscloth) needs no matching.

Doors and windows

A standard door or window doesn't remove a strip: you still hang a full drop on either side, and the short pieces above and below come from offcuts. That's why the roll count ignores them. They only change the plain area comparison, which is why their sizes are under Go advanced.

Spare strips, not a percentage

Matching waste is already counted in the strip cut length, so a flat percentage on top counts it twice. Add one to three spare strips under Go advanced instead — they cover cutting mistakes, print defects and future repairs. Buy every roll in one order so they share a dye lot.

Wallpaper sold by the yard

Trade wallcoverings, grasscloth and commercial vinyl are often sold by the yard or metre on a continuous roll. Switch Sold by to The yard / metre under Go advanced and you get the total length to order, rounded up to the next half. For paint, see the Paint Calculator.

Common questions

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need for a 12 × 14 room?

With 8 ft ceilings, a 20.5 in × 33 ft double roll and no pattern repeat, a 12 × 14 ft room needs 34 strips, which at 3 strips per roll is 12 rolls. A plain area estimate (minus one door and two windows) says about 7, which is why papering by area runs short.

How do I calculate wallpaper with a pattern repeat?

Take the wall height, add a trim allowance of about 4 inches, then round up to the next full multiple of the vertical pattern repeat. That is the cut length of every strip. Divide the roll length by the cut length to see how many strips each roll gives.

What is the difference between a straight match and a half-drop?

A straight match lines the pattern up at the same height on every strip. A half-drop offsets alternate strips by half the repeat, so every other strip needs one extra repeat of paper and the roll count rises.

How much extra wallpaper should I order?

Pattern waste is already counted in the strip cut length, so add one to three spare strips rather than a flat percentage. Spares cover cutting mistakes, print defects and future repairs. Always buy every roll from the same dye lot.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

Not for the roll count. A standard door or window doesn't remove a strip, because you still hang a full drop on either side and the short pieces above and below come from offcuts. Doors and windows only matter for the plain area comparison.
