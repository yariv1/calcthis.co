Calculators that do one thing well

Most calculator sites are enormous libraries where every tool is a compromise — a generic form bolted onto a shared template. CalcThis takes the opposite approach. Each calculator here is designed around a single real-world task, so the inputs match how you actually measure, the results come out in the units your supplier sells by, and the whole thing works the instant you type — no compute button, no account, no clutter.

Today the hub covers eight of the most-searched estimating jobs in construction and landscaping: figuring out board feet and lumber cost for a cut list, working out how much gravel a driveway or path needs in tons or bags, how much sand a paver base or leveling job takes, how much topsoil a lawn or raised bed needs by the cubic yard or bag, estimating mulch for garden beds, working out how many bags of concrete a slab or footing takes, how many boxes of flooring a room needs, and exactly how many tiles a floor, wall or backsplash takes. Each construction tool includes a running project total so you can add several boards, areas or beds and price the whole job at once — plus a waste factor so you order enough the first time. A second category covers health & fitness — with a running pace tool that solves pace, time or distance and predicts finish times, a race time predictor that projects your 5K, 10K, half and marathon times from one recent race, a heart rate zone calculator that maps your five training zones and fat-burning Zone 2, a Zone 2 heart rate calculator for your aerobic base training range, a body fat calculator using the US Navy tape method, a TDEE calculator for maintenance calories and macros, a one rep max calculator with a full training table, and a sleep cycle calculator for ideal bedtimes and wake-up times, and a macro calculator for daily protein, carb and fat targets with a live animated split ring, and a peptide reconstitution calculator that converts vial size, BAC water volume, and target dose into exact U-100 syringe draw units with a live visual syringe diagram. A third category covers school and grades: a final grade calculator that tells you exactly what score you need on your final exam to hit your target course grade, and a GPA calculator, and a weighted grade calculator that handles college 4.0 scale, high school weighted and unweighted GPAs, and AP/IB/Honors weight bumps, and a test score calculator that converts raw scores to percentages and letter grades, shows how many questions you can miss, and estimates AP scores on the 1–5 scale — twenty-four calculators in all.
