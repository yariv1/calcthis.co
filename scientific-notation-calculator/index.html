    How to use the scientific notation calculator

    Type a number into the box in any format — an ordinary number like 4500, E-notation like 2.3e11, or 3.5 × 10^-12. You can also type a whole expression such as (3×10^4)(2×10^6) or 4e5 + 7e4. The result appears in every notation at once, a picture shows the decimal point moving, and the steps are written out underneath. Use the Insert buttons for × ÷ ^ and brackets on a phone.

    What is scientific notation?

    a × 10ⁿ 1 ≤ |a| < 10 AND n IS A WHOLE NUMBER

    Scientific notation writes a number as a coefficient a between 1 and 10, multiplied by a power of ten. It makes huge and tiny numbers short and easy to compare: 6,020,000 is 6.02 × 10⁶ and 0.00042 is 4.2 × 10⁻⁴.

    How to convert a number to scientific notation

    • Move the decimal point until there is one non-zero digit to its left.
    • Count the places you moved it — that is the exponent.
    • Moved left → the exponent is positive. Moved right → it is negative.

    Example: 4,500 → move the point 3 places left → 4.5 × 10³. And 0.00042 → move it 4 places right → 4.2 × 10⁻⁴.

    Calculating with scientific notation

    Multiply: multiply the coefficients and add the exponents — (3 × 10⁴)(2 × 10⁶) = 6 × 10¹⁰. Divide: divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents — (6 × 10⁸) ÷ (3 × 10³) = 2 × 10⁵. Add or subtract: first give both numbers the same power of ten — 4 × 10⁵ + 7 × 10⁴ = 4 × 10⁵ + 0.7 × 10⁵ = 4.7 × 10⁵. The calculator does all of this for you, exactly, and shows each step.

    E-notation

    Calculators, spreadsheets and programming languages write scientific notation as E-notation: 1.5e6 means 1.5 × 10⁶ = 1,500,000. You can type it straight into the box, and every result shows its E-notation form with a Copy button.

    Scientific notation vs. standard form

    In the UK and many other countries, standard form is just another name for scientific notation. In the US it usually means an ordinary number written in full, like 4,500. This calculator shows both the scientific notation and the real number, so you can't mix them up.

    Significant figures and rounding

    If you type a single number, the calculator counts its significant figures — 0.00450 has three, 1500 has two. Under Go advanced you can round every result to a chosen number of significant figures or decimal places.

    Common questions

    What is scientific notation?

    Scientific notation writes a number as a coefficient between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of 10, in the form a × 10ⁿ. It is used for very large and very small numbers: 6,020,000 is 6.02 × 10⁶ and 0.00042 is 4.2 × 10⁻⁴.

    How do I convert a number to scientific notation?

    Move the decimal point until there is exactly one non-zero digit to its left. The number of places you moved it is the exponent: positive if you moved it left, negative if you moved it right. For 4,500 you move it 3 places left, giving 4.5 × 10³.

    How do I add numbers in scientific notation?

    Rewrite both numbers with the same power of 10, add the coefficients, then normalise the result. For example 4 × 10⁵ + 7 × 10⁴ = 4 × 10⁵ + 0.7 × 10⁵ = 4.7 × 10⁵. To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents.

    What is the difference between scientific notation and standard form?

    In the UK and many other countries, standard form is simply another name for scientific notation. In the US, standard form usually means an ordinary number written out in full, such as 4,500. This calculator shows both the scientific notation and the real number.

    What is E notation?

    E notation is the way calculators, spreadsheets and programming languages write scientific notation: the letter e stands for “times 10 to the power of”. So 1.5e6 means 1.5 × 10⁶, which is 1,500,000.
