How to use the batting average calculator

Choose Baseball / softball and enter hits and at-bats, or Cricket and enter runs scored and times out. The big number is the batting average, shown on a scale of typical ranges. Add a target average (type .300 or tap a chip) and the calculator tells you how many hits in a row you need — or, if you are already above it, how many outs in a row you can afford.

Enter the number of next at-bats you have left to see how many hits you need in them, and whether the target is still possible.

The batting average formula

Baseball: AVG = hits ÷ at-bats CRICKET: AVERAGE = RUNS SCORED ÷ TIMES OUT

120 hits in 400 at-bats is 120 ÷ 400 = .300 — "batting three-hundred". In cricket, 2,400 runs in 60 dismissals is 2,400 ÷ 60 = 40. Baseball averages are written to three decimals without the leading zero; cricket averages to two.

Hits in a row to reach a target

With 25 hits in 100 at-bats (.250) and a target of .300, each hit raises both hits and at-bats by one, so you need 8 hits in a row to get to .306. The general rule: straight hits needed = (target × at-bats − hits) ÷ (1 − target), rounded up.

OBP, slugging and OPS

Under Go advanced add walks, hit-by-pitch, sacrifice flies, doubles, triples and home runs for the fuller picture. On-base percentage = (H + BB + HBP) ÷ (AB + BB + HBP + SF); slugging = total bases ÷ AB; OPS = OBP + SLG. With 120 H, 40 BB, 5 HBP, 3 SF, 25 2B, 3 3B, 12 HR in 400 AB: OBP .368, SLG .468, OPS .836.

For cricket, the same panel takes innings and balls faced for not outs and strike rate (runs per 100 balls). For averages of any list of numbers, use the average calculator.

Common questions

How do you calculate a batting average in baseball?

Divide hits by official at-bats. 120 hits in 400 at-bats is 120 ÷ 400 = 0.300, written .300.

How do you calculate a batting average in cricket?

Divide the runs scored by the number of times out. 2,400 runs and 60 dismissals gives 2,400 ÷ 60 = 40. If a batter has never been out the average is undefined.

How many hits in a row do I need to reach a .300 average?

It depends on where you start: straight hits needed = (target × at-bats − hits) ÷ (1 − target), rounded up. From 25 hits in 100 at-bats you need 8 straight hits to reach .306. The calculator does this for any numbers.

Do walks count as at-bats?

No. Walks, hit-by-pitch and sacrifice flies are not official at-bats, so they do not change a batting average — but they do count in on-base percentage.

What is a good batting average?

In MLB, above .300 is exceptional, .250 to .300 is good to very good, and below .250 is poor to average. The last player to hit over .400 for a season was Ted Williams (.406 in 1941). In cricket, 40 to 50 is very good and above 50 is extraordinary.

Why is a batting average written without a zero?

By convention baseball averages are written to three decimals with no leading zero (.300, not 0.300) and read as "three hundred". Cricket averages are written normally, for example 40.

How is OPS calculated?

OPS is on-base percentage plus slugging percentage. Slugging is total bases divided by at-bats, where a single is 1, a double 2, a triple 3 and a home run 4. Open Go advanced to enter the extra numbers.
