How to use the paint calculator

Choose Interior or Exterior, then enter the room's length, width and height and how many doors and windows it has. Pick the number of coats and tick Ceiling or Trim & doors if you're painting them too. The room is drawn wall by wall as you type, and the shopping list shows exactly how many gallons and quarts to buy for each surface, what you'll have left over, and what it costs. Painting several rooms? Add each one — the project total combines them into a single list, so you don't buy a quart too many per room.

The paint formula

(Wall area − openings) × coats ÷ coverage = gallons WALL AREA = PERIMETER × HEIGHT · ONE GALLON COVERS ABOUT 350 SQ FT

The perimeter is twice the length plus the width. Subtract the area of every door and window, multiply by the number of coats, and divide by how far a gallon spreads. Ceilings are length × width. Trim and doors are worked out separately because they take a different paint and often a different number of coats.

Worked example: a 12 × 14 room

A 12 × 14 ft room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows, painted with two coats of wall paint:

Walls: 2 × (12 + 14) × 8 = 416 sq ft. Minus one 3 × 6.7 ft door (20 sq ft) and two 3 × 5 ft windows (30 sq ft) = 366 sq ft. Two coats = 732 sq ft ÷ 350 = 2.1 gallons, so buy 2 gal + 1 qt and have about 0.2 gal left for touch-ups.

Exterior painting

Switch to Exterior and the same maths applies to the outside walls of a house. Use Wall by wall under Go advanced to enter each side separately, including a peak height for gable ends — the triangle above the wall is added automatically. Exterior surfaces are often rougher, so set the surface texture to Rough to lower the coverage to about 300 sq ft per gallon.

How far does a gallon go?

  • Smooth walls — about 400 sq ft per gallon
  • Medium texture — about 350 sq ft per gallon
  • Rough or porous — about 300 sq ft per gallon
  • Primer — about 300 sq ft per gallon, one coat

If your can states its own coverage, type it into Coverage from your can under Go advanced and it replaces the estimate.

Primer, coats and waste

Two coats of paint is the standard. Use a coat of primer on bare drywall, patched areas, stains, glossy surfaces or when covering a dark colour with a light one — turn it on under Go advanced and it gets its own line in the shopping list. Add a small extra for waste if you're cutting in around a lot of corners or painting rough surfaces.

Trim, doors and ceilings

Tick Trim & doors to include both faces of every door, the baseboards, any crown molding and the window and door casing. The ceiling is a separate line because it's usually a flat white paint. Each can have its own number of coats under Go advanced. For the floor, see the Flooring Calculator, and use the Square Footage Calculator for odd-shaped rooms.

Common questions

How do I calculate how much paint I need?

Multiply the room's perimeter by the wall height to get the wall area, subtract doors and windows, multiply by the number of coats and divide by the paint's coverage (about 350 square feet per gallon). A 12 × 14 room with 8 ft ceilings, one door, two windows and two coats needs about 2.1 gallons of wall paint.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

A gallon covers roughly 350–400 square feet on a smooth surface for a single coat. Rough or textured surfaces soak up more paint, closer to 300 square feet per gallon. Pick the texture under Go advanced, or type in the coverage printed on your can.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes. Standard doors and windows aren't painted with wall paint, so their area is subtracted. This calculator assumes a 3 × 6.7 ft door and a 3 × 5 ft window by default and lets you change both sizes under Go advanced.

How much paint do I need for a ceiling?

The ceiling area is simply length × width. Tick Ceiling under Also paint and it gets its own line in the shopping list with its own coats, since ceilings are often painted with a different product.

Do I need primer?

Use primer on bare drywall, stains, a dark-to-light colour change or glossy surfaces. Turn on Add primer under Go advanced and the calculator adds a separate primer line, covering about 300 square feet per gallon in one coat.
