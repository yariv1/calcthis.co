How weighted grades work

Most courses divide your grade into categories — homework, quizzes, midterm, final exam — each worth a different percentage of your total grade. A weighted grade calculator multiplies each category's score by its weight, sums the results, and divides by the total weight. So a 78% midterm worth 25% contributes 19.5 percentage points to your final grade, while a 95% homework score worth 10% only contributes 9.5 points.

The formula: Weighted Grade = Σ(Score × Weight) / Σ(Weights). If your weights add to 100%, the denominator is 100 and the result is your direct course percentage. If they don't add to 100% — say your instructor uses partial weights or extra credit — the calculator still works correctly by dividing by whatever they sum to.

% Score vs Points mode

Use % Score mode when your syllabus lists category weights as percentages and you know your average score per category. Use Points mode when you track raw points — enter total points earned and total points possible per category; the calculator converts to percentages automatically.

What-if analysis

The "What Score Do I Need?" tool works backwards: given your current scores in all other categories, it calculates exactly what score you need in the selected category to hit your target course grade. Useful for planning before a final exam or major project.

Weighted grade calculator for any class or college course

A weighted grade is your course grade when some categories count for more than others. Your syllabus gives each category a share — homework 20%, quizzes 20%, midterm 25%, final exam 35% — and the calculator combines your grades with those shares. It works the same for a high school class and a college course.

How to calculate your grade with weights

Course grade = Σ (grade × weight) ÷ Σ weights EACH CATEGORY'S GRADE TIMES ITS SHARE OF THE COURSE

Take homework 90% (20%), quizzes 85% (20%) and a midterm of 78% (25%), with the final (35%) still to come. Your grade so far is (90 × 20 + 85 × 20 + 78 × 25) ÷ 65 = 83.8%. If the final comes in at 88%, the course grade is (90 × 20 + 85 × 20 + 78 × 25 + 88 × 35) ÷ 100 = 85.3%.

How to calculate your grade without weights

If every assignment counts the same, the grade is a plain average. Leave all the weight boxes empty and each category counts equally — 90, 85 and 78 average 84.3%. As soon as you type a weight, the weights take over.

What you need on the rest

To finish on a target grade T, the score needed on the remaining work is (T × total weight − points so far) ÷ remaining weight. For the example above, with a final worth 35%:

The calculator shows this table for your own numbers under “What Score Do I Need?”. Pick “All remaining” to spread the target over several categories that have no grade yet.

Letter grades

Switch to Letters if all you have are letters. Each letter counts as the middle of its band in the plus/minus table (A+ 97 · A 93 · A- 90 · B+ 87 · B 83 · B- 80 · C+ 77 · C 73 · C- 70 · D+ 67 · D 63 · D- 60): A+ is 98.5%, A- is 91%, B+ is 88%, down to F at 29.5%. A B+ and an A- of equal weight give 89.5%. Under Go advanced you can switch to the plus/minus scale for your result or set your own A, B, C and D cutoffs.

Common questions

How do I calculate a weighted grade?

Multiply each category's grade by its weight, add the results, and divide by the total weight. With homework 90% (20% of the course), quizzes 85% (20%), a midterm of 78% (25%) and a final of 88% (35%): (90 × 20 + 85 × 20 + 78 × 25 + 88 × 35) ÷ 100 = 85.3%.

How do I calculate my grade with and without weights?

With weights, bigger categories move your grade more. Without weights, every category counts the same, so the grade is a plain average: 90, 85 and 78 average 84.3%. In the calculator, leave every weight box empty and each category counts equally; fill them in and the weights take over.

What do I need on the final to get an A?

It depends on your grade so far and how much the final is worth. With homework 90% (20%), quizzes 85% (20%) and a midterm of 78% (25%), your grade so far is 83.8%. With a final worth 35%, an A (90%) would need 101.4% — out of reach — while a B (80%) needs 72.9%, a C (70%) needs 44.3% and a D (60%) needs 15.7%. Under “What score do I need?” the calculator shows every letter at once.

Can I enter letter grades instead of percentages?

Yes — switch the toggle to Letters. A letter counts as the middle of its band in the plus/minus table: A+ is 98.5%, A is 94.5%, A- is 91%, B+ is 88%, and so on down to F at 29.5%. A B+ and an A- of equal weight give 89.5%. Type percentages when you have them for an exact result.

What if my weights do not add up to 100?

The calculator scales whatever you enter, so weights of 2, 2 and 1 give the same grade as 40, 40 and 20. It shows a notice when they do not add up to 100 so you can check them against your syllabus.

How do I calculate a college or class grade?

The same way: your syllabus lists the categories (homework, labs, papers, midterm, final) and the share each one counts for. Enter those shares as weights and your grade in each category, and leave the categories you have not had yet empty. For your GPA across classes, use the GPA calculator.

How do I use points instead of percentages?

Switch to Points and enter the points you earned and the points possible for each category. A homework category of 180 out of 200 points counts as 90%. If your class is graded on total points, make each weight equal to the points possible: 180/200 and 85/100 give (180 + 85) ÷ 300 = 88.3%, the same as weighting 90% by 200 and 85% by 100.

Why is my result different from my school’s gradebook?

Common reasons: your school rounds differently, drops the lowest score in a category, adds extra credit, treats empty work as zero, or uses its own letter cutoffs (under Go advanced you can set your own A, B, C and D). For one exam use the final grade calculator; for quarters or terms, the semester grade calculator.
