    How to use the salary calculator

    Choose your currency at the top right (US dollars by default), type your pay amount and choose the period it is paid for — hour, day, week, bi-week, semi-month, month, quarter or year. The results show the same pay for every other period at once, so there is no direction to pick: hourly to salary, salary to hourly, weekly to monthly and bi-weekly to yearly are all the same box.

    Set your hours per week and days per week (40 and 5 if you leave them empty). Add the holidays and vacation days you don't get paid for to see a second column with them taken out. Open Go advanced for a raise or cut, overtime hours, your filing status and state, and to show cents. The take-home column and the tax settings are for US dollars; every other figure works in all 154 currencies.

    Hourly to salary: the formula

    Yearly pay = hourly rate × hours per week × 52 HOURLY RATE = YEARLY PAY ÷ (HOURS PER WEEK × 52)

    $25.00 an hour × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $52,000 a year, $4,333 a month, $2,000.00 every two weeks and $1,000.00 a week. Going the other way, $75,000 ÷ 2080 hours = $36.06 an hour.

    Pay periods: weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly, monthly

    There are 52 weekly paychecks in a year, 26 bi-weekly (every two weeks), 24 semi-monthly (twice a month) and 12 monthly. The yearly pay is the same, but the check size differs: $75,000 a year is $1,442.31 a week, $2,884.62 bi-weekly, $3,125.00 semi-monthly and $6,250.00 a month. A $4,000.00 bi-weekly paycheck is $104,000 a year; a $1,200.00 weekly one is $62,400; $5,000 a month is $60,000; and $200 a day over a 5-day week is $52,000.

    Holidays, vacation and unpaid time off

    Hourly workers are usually paid only for the days they work, so each unpaid holiday or vacation day takes $200.00 out of a year at $25.00 an hour and 8 hours a day. The calculator's two columns show both sides: without time off (every working day paid) and with time off (the days you enter are unpaid). A worked example: $50.00 an hour, 40 hours, 5 days, with 10 holidays and 15 vacation days is $104,000 without time off and $94,000 with it — $45.19 an hour over the whole year.

    If your holidays and vacation are paid, as they are for most salaried jobs, leave them empty or use the first column: a salary already includes them.

    From salary to take-home pay

    The take-home column runs the yearly pay through the same 2026 federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare as our paycheck calculator, with the Social Security wage limit applied exactly across 12 monthly paychecks. For $75,000 as a single filer with federal tax only that leaves $61,593 a year ($5,133 a month); with California withholding it is $57,397. Choose a state, and your filing status, under Go advanced. For exact paychecks with your W-4, deductions and any state, use the paycheck calculator or a state page.

    What a raise is worth

    Enter a percent under Go advanced to compare before and after. A 5% raise on $75,000 adds $3,750 a year before tax — about $2,638 after federal and payroll tax — and lifts the hourly figure from $36.06 to $37.86. For other percentages and the reverse (what percent is the change) use the percentage calculator.

    Overtime on top of your salary

    Hourly staff who work extra hours can add them under Go advanced: $25.00 an hour with 5 overtime hours a week at time and a half adds $9,750 over a 52-week year. Overtime rules, California's daily overtime and the no-tax-on-overtime deduction are in the overtime calculator; the hours calculator totals shifts and breaks first.

    Common questions

    How do I convert an hourly wage to a yearly salary?

    Multiply the hourly rate by the hours you work in a week and by 52. At $25.00 an hour for 40 hours a week that is $25.00 × 40 × 52 = $52,000 a year. If some holidays or vacation days are unpaid, take those days out first: the calculator shows both figures side by side.

    How do I convert a salary to an hourly rate?

    Divide the yearly salary by the hours worked in a year — 2,080 for a 40-hour week (40 × 52). A $75,000 salary is $75,000 ÷ 2080 = $36.06 an hour. Type the salary with the period set to Yearly and the hourly figure is the headline.

    What is the difference between bi-weekly and semi-monthly pay?

    Bi-weekly means every two weeks, so there are 26 paychecks a year; semi-monthly means twice a month, so there are 24. The same yearly salary therefore gives a bigger bi-weekly check: $75,000 a year is $2,884.62 bi-weekly or $3,125.00 semi-monthly. In a year with 26 bi-weekly pay dates, two months have three paychecks.

    How do holidays and vacation days change the result?

    The calculator gives two columns. "Without time off" assumes every working day is paid. "With time off" takes the holidays and vacation days you enter out as unpaid days. At $50.00 an hour with 10 holidays and 15 vacation days, $104,000 becomes $94,000 a year, which is $45.19 an hour over the whole year. If your time off is paid, as it is for most salaried jobs, use the "without time off" column.

    How many working hours and days are in a year?

    With a 5-day, 40-hour week there are 5 × 52 = 260 working days and 40 × 52 = 2080 working hours before any time off. Subtract holidays and vacation days (each day is 8 hours) to get the days you are actually paid for. Both the hours and the days a week are editable.

    How do I calculate take-home pay from a salary?

    Turn on the take-home column: it runs the pay through the same 2026 federal withholding, Social Security and Medicare as our paycheck calculator, with a state if you choose one under Go advanced. For $75,000 a year as a single filer with federal tax only the yearly take-home is $61,593 (82% kept); in Texas, which has no state income tax, it is the same $61,593, and with California withholding it is $57,397. It is an estimate: your W-4, pre-tax deductions and local taxes change it.

    How do I see what a raise is worth?

    Open Go advanced and enter the raise as a percent (or a negative number for a cut). The calculator shows hourly, monthly and yearly pay before and after, and the take-home change. A 5% raise on $75,000 is $3,750 more a year before tax and about $2,638 more after federal tax and payroll tax.

    Can I use a currency other than US dollars?

    Yes. Pick your currency at the top right of the calculator and start typing its name or code (euro, GBP, rupee, ₹) — about 154 currencies are included, with the most common ones (US, Canadian and Australian dollars, euro, pound, rupee, yen, yuan, Swiss franc, peso, real) listed first. The amounts are shown with that currency's symbol and nothing is converted, so type your pay in your own currency. The hourly, daily, weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly, monthly, quarterly and yearly figures work for any currency; the take-home column uses US federal and state tax rules, so it is shown for US dollars only.

    Does this salary calculator include overtime?

    Yes, under Go advanced: enter the extra hours you work each week and the multiplier (1.5 for time and a half). At $25.00 an hour, 5 overtime hours a week at 1.5× add $9,750 over a 52-week year. For exact overtime rules, day-by-day California pay and the no-tax-on-overtime deduction use the overtime calculator.
