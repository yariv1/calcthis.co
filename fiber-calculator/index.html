How to use the fiber calculator

Choose female or male and type your age. You get the fixed adult standard straight away. Add your daily calories — typed, or worked out from your height, weight and activity — to get the calorie-based target next to it.

Open Go advanced to track what you ate today: pick a food, type how much in servings or grams, and see the grams still to go. You can also change the 14 g per 1,000 kcal if you follow another guideline.

The formula

fiber (g) = calories ÷ 1,000 × 14 THE ADEQUATE INTAKE OF 14 G OF FIBER PER 1,000 KCAL (ACADEMY OF NUTRITION AND DIETETICS)

2,000 kcal → 28 g · 2,500 kcal → 35 g. The fixed adult standard is 25 g for women and 38 g for men.

How much fiber do I need?

The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics gives 14 g per 1,000 kcal, or 25 g for adult women and 38 g for adult men. The average US adult eats about 17 g a day, and in the 2019 Lancet analysis the risk reduction was greatest at 25–29 g a day.

Common questions

How much fiber should I eat a day?

The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics gives an Adequate Intake of 14 g of fiber per 1,000 kcal, or 25 g for adult women and 38 g for adult men. At 2,000 kcal a day the calorie-based figure is 28 g; at 2,500 kcal it is 35 g. The calculator shows both and uses the calorie-based one as your target when you add your calories.

How do I work out fiber from my calories?

Multiply your calories by 14 and divide by 1,000: 2,000 kcal × 14 ÷ 1,000 = 28 g. If you do not know your calories, choose “Work out”, enter height, weight and activity, and the calculator estimates them with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: a 35-year-old woman, 165 cm, 65 kg, moderately active comes to about 2,085 kcal and 29 g of fiber.

How much fiber does the average person eat?

In the United States the mean intake is about 17 g a day and only about 5% of people meet the Adequate Intake, according to the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics position paper. Most people therefore have a gap to close.

Does the 25 g and 38 g standard change with age?

Some tables list lower figures from age 51. I could not read a primary source for those numbers, so this calculator shows only the adult values from the Academy position paper (25 g women, 38 g men) and the calorie-based target, which scales with how much you eat.

Which foods are high in fiber?

Per 100 g, USDA lists rolled oats (dry) at 10.1 g, black beans (cooked) at 8.7 g, lentils (cooked) at 7.9 g, chickpeas (cooked) at 7.6 g, avocado at 6.7 g and raspberries at 6.5 g. A sample day of rolled oats, dry, banana, lentils, cooked, apple, with skin, broccoli, cooked, 2 × whole-wheat bread comes to 26 g.

Is more fiber better?

In the 2019 Lancet analysis the risk reduction was greatest at 25–29 g a day, and people eating the most fibre had 15–30% lower risk of several diseases than those eating the least. These are observational links, not proof that fibre alone caused the difference, and the calculator’s numbers are intakes to aim for, not treatment.

How do I use the food tally?

Open Go advanced, pick a food, type how much you ate in servings or grams and press Add food for the next. The result then shows the fiber you have had today, the share of your target and the grams still to go. For foods that are not listed, choose “My own food” and type the fiber from the label.

Do I subtract fiber to get net carbs?

Net carbs are total carbohydrate minus fiber (and some sugar alcohols). The net carb calculator does the subtraction for a food label.
