How to use it

Pick Male or Female, choose cm/kg or in/lb, then enter your age, height, current weight, activity level and a goal weight. The headline is your daily calorie target for the highlighted pace; tap another row in the pace table to switch. The curve projects your weight to the goal with dated milestones.

Tap Go advanced to plan by a target date — the calculator works out the calories that date requires and flags it if the pace is unsafe — to add your body fat % for a Katch-McArdle estimate, and to see a full protein, carb and fat split.

The method

Your maintenance calories come from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the modern standard — multiplied by an activity factor from 1.2 to 1.9. With a body fat % entered, it switches to Katch-McArdle, which uses lean body mass. To lose or gain weight, the calculator adds or subtracts a deficit based on 7,700 calories per kilogram (about 3,500 per pound).

That rule is a planning estimate. Real weight loss slows over time because a lighter body burns fewer calories, so treat the projection as a guide and recalculate every few weeks.

Losing weight safely

Common guidance is to lose about 0.5–1 kg (1–2 lb) per week and to not eat below roughly 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision. Any pace that crosses those lines is flagged in the table and in a note. A gentler deficit is easier to stick to and protects muscle.

Protein matters most in a deficit

The calculator suggests about 1.8 g of protein per kg of bodyweight. In a calorie deficit, adequate protein is what keeps the weight you lose coming from fat rather than muscle.

Common questions

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Start from your maintenance calories (Mifflin-St Jeor × activity), then subtract a deficit: about 275 kcal/day for a gentle pace, 550 for a moderate pace of ~0.5 kg/week, or up to ~1,100 for a fast pace. The pace table shows the target for each.

What is a safe rate of weight loss?

About 0.5–1 kg (1–2 lb) per week, and not below roughly 1,200 kcal/day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision. The calculator flags any pace that breaks those limits.

How long will it take to reach my goal weight?

Enter a goal weight and the calculator projects a curve to it at your chosen pace, marks the 25/50/75/100% milestones with dates, and estimates a goal date. Advanced mode lets you set a target date instead.

Is the 3,500 calories per pound rule accurate?

It's a reasonable planning estimate but it overstates long-term loss — a lighter body burns fewer calories, so progress slows. Treat the projection as a guide and re-check every few weeks.

How is this different from a TDEE calculator?

A TDEE calculator centres on maintenance calories. This one centres on a goal weight — turning it into a daily calorie number, a pace, a projected curve and a goal date. Use the TDEE calculator for a deeper macro breakdown.
