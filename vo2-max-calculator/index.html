How to use the VO2 max calculator

Choose your sex and enter your age and resting heart rate — the number of beats per minute at complete rest, most accurate first thing in the morning before getting out of bed. That's the default, zero-equipment estimate.

Tap Go advanced to switch to the 1-mile walk test instead — walk one mile at a brisk, steady pace, note your time and your heart rate immediately at the finish line, and it uses your weight for a more accurate estimate. Advanced also shows equivalent 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon race times.

What VO2 max actually measures

VO2 max is the maximum rate of oxygen your body can use during intense exercise, in millilitres per kilogram of body weight per minute (ml/kg/min). It's the standard lab measure of aerobic fitness — a higher number means your heart and lungs can deliver and your muscles can use more oxygen, which generally supports faster sustainable paces and better endurance.

Why the number alone isn't the whole story

A VO2 max of 40 means something different for a 25-year-old woman than a 60-year-old man — aerobic capacity naturally declines with age and differs by sex at every fitness level. That's why the gauge compares your result to Cooper Institute norms for your specific age band and sex, not a single universal scale.

Resting heart rate vs. the walk test

The resting heart rate method needs nothing but a quiet moment and a pulse check — quick, but a rougher estimate since it doesn't involve any actual effort. The 1-mile walk test (Kline/Rockport formula) is generally more accurate because it's based on a real physical effort, at the cost of needing a measured mile and a way to check your heart rate at the finish.

VO2 max and race pace

The advanced panel translates your estimated VO2 max into approximate finish times using the same VDOT model behind our Race Time Predictor. It's a useful rough guide, but real race performance also depends on running economy, pacing and training — not aerobic capacity alone.

Common uses

Tracking aerobic fitness over months of training, benchmarking against age-group peers, and getting a sense of what race times your current fitness could support. Pair it with the Heart Rate Zone Calculator to train at the right effort, and the Pace Calculator to plan a session around a goal pace.

Common questions

How is VO2 max estimated without a lab test?

The default method uses your resting heart rate and estimated maximum heart rate — VO2 max = 15.3 × (max heart rate ÷ resting heart rate). For a more accurate estimate, switch to the 1-mile walk test in Go advanced, which uses your weight, age, sex, walk time and finishing heart rate.

What is a good VO2 max for my age?

It depends on your age and sex — VO2 max naturally declines with age and is typically higher in men than women at the same fitness level. The gauge compares your number to Cooper Institute norms for your specific age band and sex rather than a single universal number.

How accurate is a resting heart rate VO2 max estimate?

It's a rough field estimate, not a lab measurement — useful for tracking your own trend over time rather than a precise absolute number. The 1-mile walk test method is generally more accurate since it's based on an actual physical effort rather than a resting measurement.

How does VO2 max relate to race times?

Higher aerobic capacity generally supports faster sustainable paces. The advanced panel converts your estimated VO2 max into approximate 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon times using the same VDOT model as our Race Time Predictor, though real race times also depend on running economy and training, not aerobic capacity alone.

Can I improve my VO2 max?

Yes — VO2 max responds to aerobic training, especially sustained moderate effort and interval work at high intensity. It typically improves over weeks to months of consistent training and declines gradually with age and inactivity.
