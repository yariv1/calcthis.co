How to use the mg to mL calculator

Pick what you want to find: mg to mL (the usual one), mL to mg, or the strength from a dose and a volume.

Type the dose and choose mcg, mg or g. Then type the strength on the label the way it is printed: for “250 mg per 5 mL” enter 250 in the first box and 5 in the second. If the label says “per mL”, leave the second box empty. You can also pick a label from the list (read on DailyMed) or a liquid such as water or milk.

The result shows the volume to measure on a syringe drawn to scale, in teaspoons and tablespoons, and in every other unit. Under Go advanced you can set the decimal places, the syringe size and the gap between marks on your syringe, and see how many doses a bottle holds. If your prescription gives a dose per kg of body weight, work out the dose in mg there and use it with one click.

The mg to mL formula

mL = mg ÷ (mg per mL) MILLIGRAMS ARE WEIGHT, MILLILITERS ARE VOLUME: THE STRENGTH LINKS THEM
You knowYou wantFormulaExample
Dose and strengthVolumemL = mg ÷ mg per mL500 mg ÷ 250 mg per mL = 2 mL
Volume and strengthDosemg = mL × mg per mL5 mL × 100 mg per mL = 500 mg
Dose and volumeStrengthmg per mL = mg ÷ mL200 mg ÷ 10 mL = 20 mg per mL

Without the strength there is no single answer: 1,000 mg of water is about 1 mL, but 1,000 mg of honey is 0.6979 mL and 1,000 mg of a 250 mg per mL medicine is 4 mL.

How to read a medicine label

Liquid medicines print the strength as “mg in a volume”. “250 mg/5 mL” means every 5 mL holds 250 mg, so the strength is 250 ÷ 5 = 50 mg per mL. “100 mg/5 mL” is 20 mg per mL and “50 mg/1.25 mL” is 40 mg per mL. The calculator does the division for you and shows the result as mg per mL, per 5 mL, per teaspoon and as a percentage.

Two products of the same medicine can have different strengths, so a dose that is 2.5 mL of one bottle can be a different volume of another. Always use the strength printed on the bottle in your hand.

Teaspoons, tablespoons and syringes

On medicine labels, 5 mL is 1 teaspoon, 2.5 mL is half a teaspoon and 15 mL is 1 tablespoon (MedlinePlus). A spoon from the kitchen drawer can hold anywhere from 2.5 mL to 10 mL, so use the oral syringe or cup that came with the medicine. Check that the units on it (teaspoon, tablespoon or mL) match the units of the dose, and watch the decimal point: 0.5 mL is not 5 mL. The syringe drawing on this page is schematic and to scale for the size it shows; the marks on your own syringe are what count.

Common questions

How do I convert mg to mL?

Divide the dose in mg by the strength in mg per mL. mg are weight and mL are volume, so you need the strength of the liquid. 50 mg at 20 mg per mL is 2.5 mL. For water, 1 mL weighs about 1,000 mg, so 1,000 mg is about 1 mL.

How many mL is 10 mg?

It depends on the strength. 10 mg is 10 mL at 1 mg per mL, 2 mL at 5 mg per mL, 1 mL at 10 mg per mL, 0.5 mL at 20 mg per mL, 0.2 mL at 50 mg per mL and 0.1 mL at 100 mg per mL. For water it is 0.01 mL.

How many mL is 2.5 mg?

2.5 mL at 1 mg per mL, 0.5 mL at 5 mg per mL, 0.25 mL at 10 mg per mL, 0.125 mL at 20 mg per mL and 0.05 mL at 50 mg per mL. Type your own strength in the calculator to see it on a syringe.

How many mL is 50 mg?

5 mL at 10 mg per mL, 2.5 mL at 20 mg per mL, 1 mL at 50 mg per mL, 0.5 mL at 100 mg per mL and 0.2 mL at 250 mg per mL. A 50 mg dose of children’s ibuprofen at 20 mg per mL (100 mg in 5 mL) is 2.5 mL.

How many mL is 500 mg?

5 mL at 100 mg per mL, 2 mL at 250 mg per mL, 10 mL at 50 mg per mL (250 mg in 5 mL) and 0.5 mL for water. At 1 mg per mL it would be 500 mL, which shows why the strength matters.

How do I read a label such as 250 mg/5 mL?

It means every 5 mL holds 250 mg, so the strength is 250 ÷ 5 = 50 mg per mL. Type 250 in the amount box and 5 in the liquid box, or pick the label from the list. Leave the liquid box empty when the label says “per mL”.

How do I convert mcg to mL?

Divide mcg by 1,000 to get mg, then divide by the strength. 500 mcg is 0.5 mg, which is 0.5 mL at 1 mg per mL. The calculator takes mcg, mg or g in the dose box and shows all three.

How many mL is a teaspoon or tablespoon of medicine?

Medicine labels use 1 teaspoon = 5 mL and 1 tablespoon = 15 mL (MedlinePlus). A flatware teaspoon can hold anywhere from 2.5 mL to 10 mL, so use the syringe or cup that came with the medicine.

Does this calculator tell me how much medicine to take?

No. It does the arithmetic with the numbers you type and never suggests a dose. Take the dose from your label or prescriber and ask a pharmacist if anything is unclear.
