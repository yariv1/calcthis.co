    How to use the paycheck calculator

    Choose Salary or Hourly, type your pay, pick your pay frequency, state and filing status, and your take-home pay appears as you type. For a salary you can enter the yearly amount or the amount of one paycheck. Under Go advanced you can match your Form W-4 exactly, add 401(k), insurance and other deductions, enter year-to-date pay and exempt yourself from individual taxes.

    The result shows each paycheck dollar as a bar, an itemized table per paycheck and per year, what a raise or a bigger 401(k) would change, your take-home on all ten pay schedules, and a list of the assumptions behind the numbers.

    How your paycheck is worked out

    Take-home = gross − before-tax deductions − federal tax − Social Security − Medicare − state tax − after-tax deductions 2026 TAX RATES

    On a $65,000 salary paid bi-weekly ($2,500.00 gross) for a single filer with no state income tax: federal income tax $216.15, Social Security $155.00, Medicare $36.25 — a take-home of $2,092.60 a paycheck, or $54,408 a year.

    Federal income tax and your W-4

    Your employer withholds federal income tax from the IRS percentage method in Publication 15-T. It takes your paycheck, scales it to a year, subtracts the standard-deduction-style amount built into the W-4 ($8,600 for most filers, $12,900 for married filing jointly), looks up the tax bracket and divides back to one paycheck. A married filer on the same salary has $132.31 withheld instead of $216.15. If your W-4 is from 2019 or earlier, switch to “2019 or earlier” under Go advanced and enter your allowances — each one is worth $4,300 of wages.

    Social Security and Medicare

    Social Security takes 6.2% of wages up to $184,500 in 2026 and Medicare takes 1.45% of everything, plus an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000. On a $250,000 salary paid monthly, Social Security stops after August and part of September, so take-home jumps from $14,964.25 in January to $16,068.42 in December. The chart under your result marks that paycheck.

    State income tax

    Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming have no state income tax on wages, so only federal tax and FICA apply (Washington still has employee-paid paid-family-leave and long-term-care premiums, which the calculator includes). This calculator computes state withholding from each state’s official 2026 formula for Alabama, Arizona, California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania and Virginia. For the other states the result shows federal tax and FICA only and says so — it never guesses a state rate. We are adding more states as each official formula is checked: Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Idaho, Indiana, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Mississippi, Nebraska, New Jersey, New Mexico, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Utah, Vermont, West Virginia and Wisconsin.

    City, county and school-district taxes are not included.

    Before-tax and after-tax deductions

    A traditional 401(k) lowers your federal and most state income tax but not Social Security or Medicare. Health, dental and vision premiums and HSA or FSA contributions through payroll lower both. Roth contributions and union dues come out after tax and change nothing about your taxes. With 6% of the $65,000 salary going into a 401(k), take-home falls only $132.00 a paycheck because federal tax drops by $18.00. Type 6% instead of a dollar amount to enter a share of pay.

    Hourly pay and overtime

    Switch to Hourly, enter your rate and hours per week and pick how often you are paid. At $22 an hour for 40 hours your gross is $880.00 a week. Under Go advanced, add overtime hours and the pay multiplier: 5 hours a week at 1.5× lifts gross to $1,045.00 and take-home to $881.58.

    What it does not include

    This is an estimate of withholding for 2026, not payroll or tax advice. Your employer’s payroll system, local taxes, other withholdings and your final tax return can differ. Employer-paid taxes are not part of your paycheck.

    Common questions

    How is my paycheck calculated?

    Take-home pay = gross pay − before-tax deductions − federal income tax − Social Security − Medicare − state income tax − after-tax deductions. On a $65,000 salary paid bi-weekly ($2,500.00 gross) for a single filer in a state with no income tax, federal tax is $216.15, Social Security $155.00 and Medicare $36.25, so you take home $2,092.60 a paycheck ($54,408 a year).

    How much is taken out of a $65,000 salary in California or New York?

    On the same $65,000 bi-weekly salary (single, no extra allowances), California withholds $90.01 in income tax plus $32.50 of State Disability Insurance, leaving $1,970.08 a paycheck. New York withholds $113.30 in income tax plus $12.00 of disability and paid family leave, leaving $1,967.30. Pick your state to see yours.

    Does a 401(k) lower my paycheck taxes?

    A traditional 401(k) lowers your federal and most state income tax but not Social Security or Medicare. Putting 6% of the $65,000 salary ($150.00 a paycheck) into a 401(k) lowers take-home only from $2,092.60 to $1,960.60 — $132.00 less, because federal tax drops from $216.15 to $198.15. Type “6%” in the 401(k) field under Go advanced to try it.

    When does Social Security tax stop?

    Social Security (6.2%) is withheld only on the first $184,500 of wages in 2026. On a $250,000 salary paid monthly you pay it through August and part of September, then it stops and your take-home jumps — $14,964.25 in January versus $16,068.42 in December. The yearly chart under your result marks the paycheck where it stops.

    Does the calculator work for hourly pay and overtime?

    Yes — switch to Hourly. At $22 an hour for 40 hours a week your gross is $880.00 a week ($45,760 a year) and you take home $749.00 a week as a single filer with no state tax. Under Go advanced, add overtime hours: 5 hours a week at 1.5× brings gross to $1,045.00 and take-home to $881.58.
