How to use the FFMI calculator

Pick male or female, enter your height (centimetres, inches or 5'10, metres) and weight (kg, lb or stones) and your body fat percentage. The headline is your normalized FFMI; the scale shows where it falls on the usual bands, the table below it shows the raw FFMI, your fat-free mass and fat mass in kg and lb, and a second table shows what a 3-point error in your body fat would do.

Open Go advanced and type a target FFMI to see the fat-free mass it needs at your height, how much you would have to add, and the body weight that gives.

The FFMI formula

Fat-free mass = weight × (1 − body fat %) · FFMI = fat-free mass (kg) ÷ height (m)² NORMALIZED FFMI = FFMI + 6.3 × (1.80 − HEIGHT IN M) · KOURI 1995

For a 180 cm, 75 kg man with 22% body fat: fat-free mass = 58.5 kg, FFMI = 18.1, normalized FFMI = 18.1 (the same, because he is exactly 1.80 m tall).

Don’t trust a single body-fat number

FFMI depends on a body-fat estimate that skinfolds, scales and DEXA can get several points wrong. A man of 190 cm and 90 kg scores 22.1 at 9% body fat and 20.6 at 15% — a -1.5-point spread from the estimate alone, which is why the calculator shows the ±3 point range.

Common questions

What is FFMI?

FFMI, the fat-free mass index, is your fat-free mass in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. Fat-free mass is everything that is not fat: muscle, bone, organs and water. Unlike BMI, FFMI separates fat from the rest, so extra muscle raises it while extra fat does not. It was used in Kouri et al. (1995) to compare athletes of different heights.

How do I calculate FFMI?

Fat-free mass = weight × (1 − body fat %). FFMI = fat-free mass ÷ height². For 75 kg, 180 cm and 22% body fat: fat-free mass = 75 × 0.78 = 58.5 kg; FFMI = 58.5 ÷ 1.80² = 18.1. At exactly 1.80 m the normalized FFMI is the same, 18.1. A man of 190 cm, 90 kg and 12% body fat has 79.2 kg of fat-free mass, an FFMI of 21.9 and a normalized FFMI of 21.3.

What is normalized FFMI, and why 6.3?

Fat-free mass grows faster than height squared, so taller people score higher. Kouri et al. added a correction of 6.3 × (1.80 − height in m) to put everyone on the scale of a 1.80 m man. Another calculator uses 6.1; the difference is only 0.04 of an FFMI point at 1.60 m and 0.04 at 2.00 m, but this page uses the 6.3 from the paper. For 190 cm: 21.9 + 6.3 × (1.80 − 1.90) = 21.3.

What is a good FFMI?

On Omnicalculator’s bands, which cite no study, a man is below average under 18, average at 18–20, above average at 20–22, excellent at 22–23 and superior at 23–26. For women the bands are shifted down: average 15–17, above average 17–18, excellent 18–19 and superior 19–21.5. The reference averages quoted on Menno Henselmans’ page are 18.9 for men and 15.4 for women. The bands describe a typical range; they are not a health target.

Is 25 the natural limit of FFMI?

Not as a hard ceiling. Kouri et al. (1995) found that the normalized FFMI of non-users among 157 male athletes reached a limit of 25.0, and Mr. America winners from before the steroid era averaged 25.4; the authors called this preliminary. A later study of 235 American college football players (Trexler 2017) found 26.4% above 25, a 97.5th percentile of 28.1 and a maximum of 31.7, although that abstract does not say the players were drug tested. A value above 25 is unusual in the drug-free samples, not proof of anything about one person.

What FFMI do women have?

Women carry less fat-free mass per height squared, so their scale sits lower; the average quoted on Menno Henselmans’ page is 15.4 against 18.9 for men, and Omnicalculator’s bands run about 3 points lower. The 25.0, 25.4 and 28.1 reference points come from male athletes and are not shown for women. I did not find a women’s study I could read, so the calculator gives women the bands and the average only.

How accurate is an FFMI calculation?

Only as accurate as the body-fat percentage you enter. Skinfolds, bioimpedance scales and DEXA can differ by several points. Try it: 90 kg at 190 cm gives a normalized FFMI of 21.3 at 12% body fat, 22.1 at 9% and 20.6 at 15%. The calculator shows this ±3 point range for your own numbers.

What is the difference between FFMI and BMI?

BMI divides total weight by height squared, so muscle and fat count the same. FFMI divides only the fat-free part, which is why a muscular person can have an “overweight” BMI and a normal body fat. For the 190 cm, 90 kg man above BMI is 24.9; FFMI is 21.9. See the BMI calculator and the body fat calculator for the body-fat figure you need.

How much fat-free mass is a target FFMI?

Under Go advanced enter a target normalized FFMI. At 180 cm a normalized FFMI of 22 needs 71.3 kg of fat-free mass and 25 needs 81.0 kg. The calculator subtracts what you have and shows what is left to add, and the body weight that would give at your body fat or one you choose. It makes no claim about how long that takes.
