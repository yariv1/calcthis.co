How to use the A1C calculator

Type your A1C in % or in mmol/mol, or an average glucose in mg/dL or mmol/L. Whichever you type, the other three fill in — there is no mode to switch. Under the result you get a scale that marks your number against the normal, prediabetes and diabetes ranges, with your goal marker, and a line telling you where it falls.

Enter an average glucose and you also see the GMI — the estimate used for continuous glucose monitor (CGM) data — beside the ADAG-formula A1C. Under Go advanced you can set your own goal or paste a list of readings and let the calculator average them.

The A1C to eAG formula

eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7 ESTIMATED AVERAGE GLUCOSE · ADAG STUDY

For an A1C of 7.0%: 28.7 × 7 − 46.7 = 154 mg/dL, or 8.6 mmol/L (divide by 18.016). Backwards, (154 + 46.7) ÷ 28.7 = 7.0%. In mmol/mol the same 7.0% is 53: (A1C − 2.15) × 10.929.

A1C, eAG and GMI — not the same thing

A1C is a lab test of glycated hemoglobin. eAG is that A1C written in glucose units. GMI is computed from a CGM's average glucose with a different formula, fitted to sensor data. They agree near 154 mg/dL and drift apart elsewhere:

A single result does not make a diagnosis; it has to be a laboratory test and is normally repeated. The ADA's common goal for adults with diabetes is 7% or lower, but personal goals vary with age, pregnancy and other factors.

Why your meter and your A1C disagree

A1C averages roughly two to three months of glucose, while a meter shows moments. The formula is an average over many people (R² 0.84), not an exact rule for one person, and swings up and down can average out to a normal-looking A1C. Paste your readings under Go advanced to see their average against your lab result.

Common questions

How do I convert A1C to average blood sugar?

Multiply the A1C by 28.7 and subtract 46.7: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7. An A1C of 7.0% gives 28.7 × 7 − 46.7 = 154 mg/dL; divide by 18.016 for mmol/L, 8.6. This is the relationship from the ADAG study that the American Diabetes Association uses.

How do I convert average blood sugar to A1C?

Add 46.7 to the average glucose (mg/dL) and divide by 28.7. An average of 154 mg/dL gives (154 + 46.7) ÷ 28.7 = 7.0%. For mmol/L, multiply by 18.016 first: 8.6 mmol/L is 155 mg/dL, an A1C of 7.0%. Type either number into the calculator and the other fills in.

What is a normal A1C?

Below 5.7% is normal, 5.7% to 6.4% is the prediabetes range and 6.5% or higher is the diabetes range — a 5.7% A1C is about 117 mg/dL and a 6.5% A1C about 140 mg/dL. A diagnosis needs a laboratory test, repeated if abnormal; a calculator cannot diagnose.

What is the average blood sugar for an A1C of 7?

About 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L), or 53 mmol/mol in the newer international units. The calculator also shows where 7% sits on a scale next to the normal, prediabetes and diabetes ranges.

What is A1C in mmol/mol?

Many countries report A1C as mmol/mol (IFCC units) instead of %. mmol/mol = (A1C% − 2.15) × 10.929, so 7.0% is 53 mmol/mol and 6.5% is 48 mmol/mol. Type either unit and the calculator converts.

What is the difference between A1C, eAG and GMI?

A1C is a lab blood test (a percentage of glycated hemoglobin). eAG is that A1C expressed in glucose units using the ADAG formula. GMI is a different estimate worked out from the average glucose of a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) — GMI = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean glucose in mg/dL. The two formulas cross near 154 mg/dL and drift apart elsewhere: at 250 mg/dL the ADAG formula gives 10.3% and GMI 9.3%. Enter an average glucose and the calculator shows both; the GMI calculator goes deeper.

Why does my meter average not match my A1C?

A1C reflects roughly the last two to three months, because red blood cells live about three months, while a meter shows single moments. The formula is also an average across many people (R² 0.84), not an exact rule for one person, and frequent highs and lows can average out to a normal-looking A1C. A gap is common — discuss it with your care team.

Can I enter a list of readings?

Yes. Open Go advanced, choose mg/dL or mmol/L and paste your readings, one per line or separated by commas. The calculator averages them and fills in the estimated A1C and GMI. It ignores anything that is not a plausible glucose value and tells you how many it ignored.

Can this calculator diagnose diabetes?

No. It converts numbers using published formulas and shows which range a value falls in. Diagnosis and treatment decisions need a laboratory test and your healthcare professional.
