    How to use the amortization calculator

    Type the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the term (years, months, or both like 5y 6m; weeks and days work too). The result shows the payment, the total interest and a chart that stacks every payment into the part that is interest and the part that reduces the loan. Drag the slider under the chart, or type a payment number, a month and year or a date in Look at one point in the loan, to see the balance, the interest paid so far and the interest still to pay at that point.

    Type an extra payment to see how much sooner the loan is done and how much interest you save, or click a row of the Pay it off faster table. Go advanced for the price and down payment, how often you pay (ten options, daily to yearly), how often the interest is added, the loan start date, an extra amount once a year and one-time extra payments on a date.

    The formula

    A = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n) BALANCE AFTER A PAYMENT = BALANCE × (1 + i) − A

    A is the payment, P the loan, i the interest rate for one payment period (the yearly rate divided by the payments in a year) and n the number of payments. The first payment is due one full period after the loan starts. $200,000.00 at 6% for 15 years: i = 6% ÷ 12 = 0.5% and n = 180, so A = $1,687.71. At 0% the payment is just P ÷ n.

    What a schedule shows

    Each payment is the same, but its parts change. The first six payments of $300,000.00 at 6.5% over 30 years:

    PaymentInterestPrincipalBalance
    1$1,625.00$271.20$299,728.80
    2$1,623.53$272.67$299,456.12
    3$1,622.05$274.15$299,181.97
    4$1,620.57$275.64$298,906.34
    5$1,619.08$277.13$298,629.21
    6$1,617.57$278.63$298,350.58

    The interest in the first payment is the balance times the rate for one period: $300,000.00 × 6.5% ÷ 12 = $1,625.00. The rest of the $1,896.20, $271.20, reduces the loan, so next month's interest is a little smaller and the principal part a little bigger. From payment 233 the principal part is the larger one.

    What extra payments do

    Extra money goes straight to the balance, so the next interest is charged on less. On the $300,000.00 loan at 6.5% over 30 years:

    Extra each monthPaid off inInterest saved
    None30 years—
    $10026 years$60,995
    $20023 years 1 month$103,449
    $50017 years 6 months$179,759

    A one-time $10,000.00 extra payment at payment 12 saves $50,230 of interest and shortens the loan to 27 years 5 months.

    Paying more often

    The payment is worked out for the frequency you pick, with the same term. $300,000.00 at 6.5% over 30 years:

    You payPaymentTotal interest

    Paying every two weeks with a payment calculated for 26 payments a year is not the same as paying half the monthly payment every two weeks, which makes one extra monthly payment a year. To test that, keep the frequency on monthly and type half the monthly payment as the extra once a year.

    Common questions

    What is an amortization schedule?

    It is a table of every payment on a loan that is paid off in equal instalments. Each row shows how much of the payment is interest, how much reduces the loan and what balance is left. For $200,000.00 at 6% over 15 years the payment is $1,687.71 and the first payment is $1,000.00 interest and $687.71 principal.

    How do I calculate an amortized loan payment?

    Use A = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), where P is the loan, i the interest rate for one payment period and n the number of payments. For $300,000.00 at 6.5% over 30 years, i = 6.5% ÷ 12 and n = 360, so the payment is $1,896.20. At 0% the payment is simply the loan divided by the number of payments.

    Why is so much of my first payment interest?

    Interest is charged on the balance still owed, which is largest at the start. On $300,000.00 at 6.5% the first payment of $1,896.20 is $1,625.00 interest and only $271.20 principal. From payment 233 more of each payment goes to principal than to interest.

    How much do I still owe after a few years?

    Drag the slider or type a payment number, a month and year or a date. After 5 years on the $300,000.00 loan at 6.5% over 30 years you still owe $280,832.93, after 10 years $254,328.38 and after 15 years $217,677.42.

    How do extra payments change an amortization schedule?

    An extra payment goes straight to the balance, so the next interest is charged on less. Adding $200.00 a month to the $300,000.00 loan at 6.5% pays it off in 23 years 1 month instead of 30 years and saves $103,449 of interest. You can add an extra amount to every payment, once a year, or as one-time payments on a date.

    Can I make a one-time extra payment?

    Yes. Open Go advanced, add a one-time payment and type the amount and when: a month and year like Mar 2030, a date, or a payment number like 24. A single $10,000.00 payment at payment 12 on the same loan saves $50,230 of interest.

    Does paying every two weeks or every week change the schedule?

    Yes. The payment is worked out for the frequency you choose, with the same term: $300,000.00 at 6.5% over 30 years costs $382,633 of interest paid monthly and $382,312 paid every two weeks (26 smaller payments a year). To test paying half the monthly payment every two weeks, which makes one extra monthly payment a year, keep the frequency on monthly and type half the monthly payment as the yearly extra.

    What is the difference between a 15-year and a 30-year loan?

    The shorter loan has a higher payment but far less interest: $300,000.00 at 6.5% costs $2,613.32 a month and $170,398 of interest over 15 years, against $1,896.20 and $382,633 over 30 years.

    Can I use it for a mortgage or a car loan?

    Yes, for any loan with a fixed rate and equal payments: mortgage, auto loan, personal or student loan. Under Go advanced you can type the price and down payment instead of the loan amount. Taxes, insurance and fees are not included; use the mortgage or auto loan calculator for those.

    Does the calculator work at 0% interest?

    Yes. At 0% every payment is all principal: $12,000.00 over 24 months is $500.00 a month with no interest.

    Does the calculator work in my currency?

    Choose your currency at the top right of the calculator: the amounts show in it with the symbol on the usual side. Nothing is converted, only the display changes.
