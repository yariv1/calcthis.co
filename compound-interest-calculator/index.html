    How to use the compound interest calculator

    Type your starting amount, a regular deposit, the yearly interest rate and the time (years, months, or both like 5y 6m) and choose how often the interest is added. The result shows the balance, how much you put in and how much the interest adds, with a chart that stacks them and a dashed line for the same money without compounding.

    Under What do you want to find? choose Deposit needed, Starting amount needed, Time needed or Rate needed, type the amount you want (or the interest you want to earn), and the hidden box is solved for you. Go advanced for deposits every week or year, deposits at the start of the period, a deposit that grows with your pay, tax on the interest, inflation, a lower and a higher rate, and money taken out later.

    The formula

    A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t) CONTINUOUS: A = P × e^(r × t) · EFFECTIVE YEARLY RATE: (1 + r ÷ n)^n − 1

    P is the amount you start with, r the yearly rate as a decimal, n the number of times a year interest is added and t the years. $10,000.00 at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years: $10,000.00 × (1 + 0.05 ÷ 12)^120 = $16,470.09, which is $6,470.09 of interest. Regular deposits grow the same way, each for the time it stays invested.

    Simple interest versus compound interest

    Simple interest is paid only on the original amount. $1,000.00 at 10% for 2 years earns $200.00 in interest with simple interest and $210.00 when compounded yearly: the second year's interest is paid on the first year's interest too. The longer the time, the bigger the gap, which is why the chart draws the money without compounding as a dashed line.

    Reaching a goal

    To reach $100,000.00 in 20 years at 5% compounded monthly, starting from nothing, you need $243.29 a month ($58,389.38 put in, $41,610.62 interest). Starting with $50,000.00 and adding $1,000.00 a month at 7%, you pass $1,000,000.00 after 23 years 11 months. To turn $10,000.00 into $20,000.00 in 10 years you need about 6.9515% a year. Choose what to find at the top of the calculator and type the goal.

    The rule of 72

    Dividing 72 by the yearly rate estimates the years it takes to double your money. The rule is attributed to Luca Pacioli (1494). Against the exact answer for yearly compounding:

    Yearly rateExact doubling timeRule of 72
    2%35.00 years36.00 years
    4%17.67 years18.00 years
    6%11.90 years12.00 years
    8%9.01 years9.00 years
    10%7.27 years7.20 years
    12%6.12 years6.00 years

    Tax and inflation

    If the interest is taxed as it is earned, only part of it grows. $10,000.00 at 5% for 10 years becomes $16,470.09 with no tax and $14,908.33 with a 20% tax on the interest. Inflation shrinks what the money buys: with prices rising 2.5% a year, $16,470.09 in 10 years buys what $12,866.41 buys today.

    Common questions

    How do I calculate compound interest?

    Multiply the amount by (1 + rate ÷ n) raised to n × years, where n is how many times a year the interest is added: A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t). $10,000.00 at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years is $10,000.00 × (1 + 0.05 ÷ 12)^120 = $16,470.09. Regular deposits are added the same way and grow too, which the calculator does for you.

    What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

    Simple interest is paid only on the original amount; compound interest is also paid on the interest already earned. $1,000.00 at 10% for 2 years gives $1,200.00 with simple interest and $1,210.00 compounded yearly. The chart shows the same money without compounding as a dashed line.

    How much will I have if I save a set amount every month?

    Type the starting amount, the monthly deposit, the rate and the years. $10,000.00 plus $200.00 a month at 6% compounded monthly becomes $125,510.22 after 20 years: you put in $58,000.00 and the interest adds $67,510.22.

    How much do I need to save each month to reach a goal?

    Choose “Deposit needed” under What do you want to find, type the amount you want, the rate and the years. To reach $100,000.00 in 20 years at 5% compounded monthly from nothing you need $243.29 a month: $58,389.38 put in and $41,610.62 of interest.

    How long will it take to reach a goal?

    Choose “Time needed”. Starting with $50,000.00 and adding $1,000.00 a month at 7% compounded monthly, you pass $1,000,000.00 after 23 years 11 months.

    How much do I need to start with to reach a goal?

    Choose “Starting amount needed”, type the amount you want, the rate and the years. To have $50,000.00 in 10 years at 5% compounded monthly, with no deposits, you need $30,358.05 today; the interest makes up the other $19,641.95.

    How much do I need to save to earn a set amount of interest?

    Open Go advanced and set “The amount I want is” to the interest earned. To earn $10,000.00 of interest in 10 years at 5% compounded monthly you would save $283.43 a month: $34,011.41 put in and $10,000.00 of interest.

    What rate do I need to reach a goal?

    Choose “Rate needed”. To turn $10,000.00 into $20,000.00 in 10 years with monthly compounding you need about 6.9515% a year.

    Does compounding more often make a big difference?

    It helps a little: $10,000.00 at 5% for 10 years gives $16,288.95 compounded yearly, $16,470.09 monthly and $16,486.65 daily. The table under the result compares all frequencies for your own numbers.

    How long does it take to double my money?

    The rule of 72 divides 72 by the yearly rate: at 6% about 12 years. The exact figure at 6% compounded yearly is 11.90 years. The result table shows both for your rate.

    How do taxes and inflation change the result?

    In Go advanced, type the tax on interest and the inflation rate. Tax is taken from the interest as it is earned, so 5% with a 20% tax grows like 4%: $10,000.00 becomes $14,908.33 instead of $16,470.09 in 10 years. Inflation of 2.5% means $16,470.09 will buy what $12,866.41 buys today.

    Can I see how long my money lasts when I take it out?

    Yes. Go advanced and type a monthly withdrawal. After the saving years the calculator takes that amount out every month and shows how long the money pays in full, or that it never runs out when the interest covers the withdrawals.

    Does the calculator work in my currency?

    Choose your currency at the top right of the calculator: the amounts show in it with the symbol on the usual side. Nothing is converted, only the display changes.
