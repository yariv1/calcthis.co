How to use the dog food calculator

Enter your dog’s weight (or pick a breed for a typical adult weight), say whether it is a puppy, adult or senior, and for an adult whether it is neutered. The headline is the recommended calories a day. Then choose the type of food: dry kibble or wet (canned) uses a typical calorie value and gives the cups or cans a day and per meal; My own lets you type the calories from your bag or can for an exact amount. Pick a treat share if you give treats.

Open Go advanced for a more specific type of dog (weight loss, working, pregnant…), your own cup size in ml, your own factor from your vet, or to split the calories between two foods, such as kibble and wet or raw.

Adjust from your dog, not the number

Both sources say the figure is an estimate. If your dog is gaining or losing weight, change the amount and check again in a couple of weeks; for puppies, recalculate as they grow. For a dog that needs to lose weight, or one with a health condition, ask your vet to set the plan. See also the dog age calculator.

Common questions

How much should I feed my dog?

Start from calories: a dog’s resting energy requirement is 70 × weight in kg0.75, and the daily need is that times a factor for its life stage and activity. For a 20 kg neutered adult that is 662 kcal at rest and about 927–1,059 kcal a day. Then divide by the calories in your food — a food with 350 kcal per cup means about 3 cups a day. Weigh your dog every couple of weeks and adjust from the trend.

How many calories does a dog need a day?

It depends on weight and life stage. At 5, 10, 20, 30 and 40 kg a neutered adult needs about 374, 630, 1,059, 1,436, 1,781 kcal a day (factor 1.6). Intact adults use 1.6–1.8 times the resting need and sedentary or overweight-prone dogs 1.0–1.4, so the same dog can need 662–1,192 kcal a day at 20 kg depending on the type.

How do I work out cups of dog food?

Divide the daily calories by the calories in a cup of your food. Dry food typically has about 350 kcal per US cup (two labels I read: Hill’s Science Diet Adult 365, Royal Canin Medium Adult 340), so 1,059 ÷ 350 = 3.03 cups, shown as about 3 cups. The calculator does this for you with a typical value; choose “My own” and type the calories from your bag for an exact figure, because foods differ. A cup is a US 8 fl oz cup (237 ml) unless you enter your own scoop size under Go advanced.

How much should a puppy eat?

Puppies use more calories for their weight: the factor is 3.0 times the resting need under 4 months and 2.0 from 4 months (APOP and Omnicalculator agree). An 8 kg puppy needs about 999 kcal a day under 4 months and 666 kcal from 4 months. The calculation uses the puppy’s current weight, so recalculate as it grows; enter the age in months and the right factor is picked for you.

How much should I feed a dog that needs to lose weight?

Veterinary guidance gives 1.0–1.2 times the resting need for weight-loss planning; this calculator uses 1.0 for the headline and shows the range. For a 30 kg dog that is about 897–1,077 kcal a day. A weight-loss plan needs a vet to set the target weight and recheck it, so treat this as a starting figure.

How many treats can my dog have?

APOP says treats should stay at or below 10% of daily calories and be subtracted from the daily amount rather than added. For the 20 kg neutered dog, 10% is about 106 kcal, so the food drops to about 953 kcal. Pick 5% or 10% in the calculator and it does the subtraction.

Why does the calculator show a range?

Dogs of the same weight differ in activity, age, coat, breed and metabolism, and the two sources give factor ranges rather than single values for adults. A range is more honest than one exact number; the headline uses the single factor Omnicalculator gives and the range comes from the APOP tool.

Is the raw food rule of 2–3% of body weight right?

Raw-food sellers often quote 2–3% of body weight a day for adult dogs. That figure came from commercial pages, not from a veterinary source I could read, so it is shown only as a comparison under Go advanced. Raw and fresh foods differ a lot in calories per kg and I found no verified typical value, so choose “My own” and enter the kcal per kg from the label.

Can I feed two foods, like kibble and wet?

Yes. Under Go advanced add a second food with its calories and the share of the food calories it should supply; the calculator then gives the amount of each. The first food is whichever type you picked, dry, wet or your own. Treats are taken off the top first.

Does this work for senior dogs?

It has a senior type with a factor of 1.1 times the resting need, taken from Omnicalculator; the APOP tool does not give a separate senior factor. Older dogs vary a lot, so use your vet and the weight trend to adjust. See the dog age calculator for when a dog counts as senior.
