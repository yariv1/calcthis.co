How to use the auto loan calculator

Enter the vehicle price, the interest rate and the loan term in months — or tap 36, 48, 60, 72 or 84. Then add your down payment, any cash incentives, your trade-in and what you still owe on it, the sales tax rate and the title, registration and other fees. The result updates as you type: your monthly payment, what you pay at signing, where your money goes, how the balance falls and a full amortization schedule.

Not sure what to shop for? Switch to Max car price, enter the monthly payment that fits your budget, and the calculator works backwards to the car price it covers.

How the amount financed is worked out

Loan = price − incentives − down payment − trade-in + owed on trade-in ADD SALES TAX AND FEES TO THE LOAN IF YOU ROLL THEM IN

For a $35,000 car with $5,000 down, an $8,000 trade-in that still owes $3,000, 7% sales tax and $500 of fees: the trade-in is worth $5,000 net, so the loan is $35,000 − $5,000 − $8,000 + $3,000 = $25,000. At 6.5% over 60 months the payment is $489.15 a month, with $4,349.22 of interest. You also pay $7,390 at signing: the $5,000 down plus $1,890 of sales tax and $500 of fees.

Trade-ins, sales tax and negative equity

In most states the trade-in lowers the amount that is taxed, so the $35,000 car above is taxed on $27,000 rather than the full price. If you owe more on your trade-in than it is worth, the shortfall — negative equity — is added to your new loan. A $9,000 car that still owes $12,000 adds $3,000 to the loan. The calculator flags it, and under Go advanced you can switch the tax rule to “Full price” if your state gives no trade-in credit.

Should taxes and fees be in the loan?

Ticking “Include taxes and fees in the loan” lowers what you pay at signing but raises the loan and the interest. On the example, rolling in $2,390 of tax and fees cuts the signing cost from $7,390 to $5,000, but the payment rises from $489.15 to $535.92 and you pay $415.79 more interest.

Shorter or longer?

A shorter loan costs more each month but far less in total. On the $25,000 loan at 6.5%, 36 months is $766.23 a month with $2,584 of interest; 60 months is $489.15 with $4,349; 84 months is $371.24 with $6,184. The table under every result compares 36, 48, 60, 72 and 84 months for your own numbers.

How much car can I afford?

Start from your budget instead of the sticker price. With $5,000 down, 7% tax and $500 of fees, a $500 monthly payment at 6.5% supports a car around $30,554 over 60 months, $34,744 over 72 months and $38,671 over 84 months. A longer term lets the same payment reach a pricier car, but you pay interest for longer.

Paying it off faster and watching the value

Under Go advanced, type an extra monthly amount to see how much interest it saves — adding $50 a month to the $25,000 loan finishes it 6 months sooner and saves $481. You can also enter how much value the car loses each year; the balance chart then draws the car’s value beside your loan and shows how long you would owe more than the car is worth.

Common questions

How is the amount financed on a car loan calculated?

Amount financed = vehicle price − cash incentives − down payment − trade-in value + amount owed on the trade-in (+ sales tax and fees if you roll them in). For a $35,000 car with $5,000 down and an $8,000 trade-in that still owes $3,000, the trade-in is worth $5,000 net, so you finance $25,000. At 6.5% over 60 months that is $489.15 a month and $4,349.22 of interest.

Does my trade-in lower the sales tax?

In most states it does: tax is charged on the price minus the trade-in. With 7% tax, the $35,000 car taxes only $27,000 after the $8,000 trade-in, so the tax is $1,890. If your state taxes the full price, the tax is $2,450 instead. This calculator uses price minus trade-in by default; switch to “Full price” under Go advanced if your state does not give the credit. Check your state’s rule — it varies.

What if I owe more on my trade-in than it is worth?

That shortfall is called negative equity, and it is added to your new loan. Trading in a car worth $9,000 that still owes $12,000 adds $3,000 to the loan on top of the price, so with $2,000 down you finance $36,000 and pay $704.38 a month. The calculator flags the shortfall whenever it happens.

Should I roll taxes and fees into the loan?

Rolling them in lowers what you pay at signing but raises the loan. On the example above the tax and fees come to $2,390. Paying them upfront means $7,390 at signing and a $489.15 payment; rolling them in means $5,000 at signing, a $27,390 loan, a $535.92 payment and $415.79 more interest. Tick “Include taxes and fees in the loan” to compare.

How much car can I afford for a $500 monthly payment?

Switch to “Max car price”. With $5,000 down, 7% tax, $500 of fees and a 6.5% rate, a $500 payment supports a car priced around $30,554 over 60 months, $34,744 over 72 months and $38,671 over 84 months. A longer loan raises the price you can afford, but you pay more interest, so check the term table too.
