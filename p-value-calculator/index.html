How to use the p-value calculator

Choose the statistic your test gave you — z, t, chi-square, F, or a correlation r — and type its value. t, chi-square, F and r also need degrees of freedom (or the sample size for r); the note under each field says where to find them. Type your significance level α (0.05 is used if you leave it empty) and choose the type of p-value: two-tailed for any difference, left-tailed for “lower than”, right-tailed for “higher than”.

The result shows the p-value for that tail, whether it is at or below α, and a curve of the distribution with your statistic marked and the p-value area shaded. The table under it gives the left, right and two-tailed p-values at once; click a row to switch tails. Go advanced to turn a p-value (or an area between −x and x, or from the centre to x, for z, t and r) back into a statistic, or to work out t or z from sample means and standard deviations.

How to read the p-value

If the p-value is at or below your significance level α, the result is statistically significant and you reject the null hypothesis; if it is above α you do not have enough evidence to reject it. Rejecting or not rejecting says nothing about how big or useful the effect is, and a p-value above α does not prove the null hypothesis true. Choose α before you look at the data.

Worked example

A t-test gives t = 2.1 with 20 degrees of freedom. Two-tailed: p = 0.04862, below 0.05, so the difference is significant at the 5% level. Right-tailed: p = 0.02431. For r = 0.45 from 25 pairs, t = 2.417 with 23 degrees of freedom and a two-tailed p-value of 0.024.

Common questions

How do I find a p-value from a test statistic?

Pick the statistic (z, t, chi-square, F or r), type its value and, if it has them, the degrees of freedom, then choose the tail. For t = 2.1 with 20 degrees of freedom the two-tailed p-value is 0.04862 and the right-tailed one is 0.02431.

What is a p-value?

The p-value is the probability, if the null hypothesis were true, of getting a test statistic at least as extreme as the one you observed. A small p-value means your result would be unusual under the null hypothesis. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

What is the difference between a one-tailed and a two-tailed p-value?

A two-tailed test looks for a difference in either direction and counts both tails of the curve; a right-tailed test asks whether the value is higher than the reference, a left-tailed one whether it is lower. For the symmetric z and t distributions the two-tailed p-value is twice the smaller tail: z = 1.96 gives 0.025 in the right tail and 0.05 for two tails.

What p-value is statistically significant?

Compare it with the significance level α you chose before the test. If p ≤ α you reject the null hypothesis. 0.05 is the most common choice, and 0.01 and 0.10 are also used, but none of them is a law of nature.

How do I find the p-value from a t-score?

Choose t-score, type the t value and the degrees of freedom (n − 1 for one sample or a paired test, n₁ + n₂ − 2 for two samples with equal variance). t = 2.3 with 11 degrees of freedom gives a two-tailed p-value of 0.04203.

How do I find the p-value from a z-score?

Choose z-score and type it; no degrees of freedom are needed. z = 1.96 gives 0.05 for a two-tailed test, and z = −1.5 gives 0.06681 in the left tail.

How do I find the p-value for a chi-square test or an F test?

Choose chi-square or F, type the value and the degrees of freedom (F needs two: numerator and denominator). These tests almost always use the right tail: χ² = 3.84 with 1 degree of freedom gives 0.05004, and F = 4.96 with 1 and 10 degrees of freedom gives 0.05009.

Can I get the p-value from a correlation coefficient?

Yes. Choose correlation r and type r and the number of pairs n. The calculator turns r into t = r × √(df ÷ (1 − r²)) with df = n − 2: r = 0.45 with n = 25 gives t = 2.417 and a two-tailed p-value of 0.024.

Can I find the test statistic from a p-value?

Yes. Go advanced and type the p-value in “Find the statistic from a p-value”: you get the z, t, chi-square, F or r that produces it for the tail you chose. A two-tailed p-value of 0.05 with z gives ±1.96.

Can a p-value be exactly 0?

In practice no. Very large statistics give extremely small p-values, which this calculator shows in scientific notation such as 1.524 × 10⁻²³ for z = 10, rather than rounding to 0.
