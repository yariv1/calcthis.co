    How to use the overtime calculator

    Enter your hourly rate, then choose how to give the hours. Total hours works out the overtime for you from the hours after which it starts (40 a week unless you change it). Regular + overtime takes the two numbers as you have them on a pay stub. Day by day takes each day's hours and applies the federal weekly rule or California's daily rules.

    Pick the multiplier — 1.5 for time and a half, 2 for double time, or your own — and you get the total pay, a picture of how the hours and the pay split, the take-home after tax, and what the no-tax-on-overtime deduction could save you. Open Go advanced to add your state and filing status, a bonus, rounding to 5, 6 or 15 minutes, and the weeks you work like this.

    The overtime pay formula

    Total pay = regular hours × rate + overtime hours × rate × multiplier TIME AND A HALF = 1.5 · DOUBLE TIME = 2

    At $22.00 an hour and 48 hours in the week: regular 40 × $22.00 = $880.00; overtime 8 × ($22.00 × 1.5) = 8 × $33.00 = $264.00; total $1,144.00. At double time the same week is $1,232.00.

    Federal overtime: the 40-hour rule

    The federal Fair Labor Standards Act requires overtime pay at not less than time and a half the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, for covered employees. Some employees are exempt from the rule, and your employer may pay more than the minimum. A workweek is a fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 hours — seven consecutive 24-hour periods — that can start on any day, and hours are never averaged over two weeks. Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Fact Sheet #23 (revised October 2019).

    California overtime: daily, weekly and the seventh day

    California adds daily rules: 1.5× the regular rate after 8 hours in a day up to 12, 2× after 12, and on the seventh consecutive day of the workweek 1.5× for the first 8 hours and 2× after that. The calculator applies them in Day by day with the California rule, and counts hours over 40 regular hours a week at 1.5× as well.

    Example at $22.00 an hour — 9, 10, 8, 13, 8 hours Monday to Friday: California pays 40 regular + 7 at 1.5× + 1 at 2× = $1,155.00; the federal weekly rule pays 40 regular + 8 at 1.5× = $1,144.00. Working eight hours six days and ten on the seventh gives 40 regular + 16 at 1.5× + 2 at 2× = $1,496.00 in California. Source: California Labor Commissioner's Office. Other states with their own daily rules are not built in — check your state labor department.

    Take-home pay on overtime

    The take-home table runs the week's check through the same federal and state withholding as our paycheck calculator, with and without the overtime, so you see what the extra hours add after tax. For the 48-hour example that is $1,144.00 of pay and $961.13 take-home as a single filer with federal tax only — $212.12 of the $264.00 overtime pay (80%). With Texas (no state income tax) it is $961.13, and with California withholding $908.99.

    Overtime is not taxed at a special rate. Payroll treats a big week's check as if every paycheck were that large, so withholding can be higher than the tax you owe for the year; the difference comes back when you file.

    The no tax on overtime deduction

    For tax years 2025 through 2028 you can deduct the overtime pay that is more than your regular rate and that federal law requires — in time and a half, the extra half. The most you can deduct is $12,500 a year ($25,000 on a joint return), reduced by $100 for each $1,000 of modified adjusted gross income over $150,000 ($300,000 joint). You need a valid Social Security number, married people must file jointly, and you can take it with the standard deduction or itemizing.

    ExampleDeductionFederal tax saved
    $22.00 an hour, 48 hours a week, single, 52 weeks$4,576$549
    $60.00 an hour, 60 hours a week, single, 52 weeks$5,660$1,400

    It lowers federal income tax only: Social Security and Medicare are still taken from the whole check, and state tax is not changed here. The estimate takes your income as this week's pay × the weeks you enter unless you type your own, and uses the 2026 standard deduction and tax brackets. You claim it on Schedule 1-A; for 2025 employers did not have to show it separately on your W-2, and the IRS explains how to work it out from your pay records. Sources: IRS: What to know about the No Tax on Overtime deduction (March 5, 2026) and 26 U.S.C. § 225.

    Bonuses and the regular rate

    A non-discretionary bonus counts as part of your regular rate, so overtime is owed on it. Under Go advanced, the bonus is divided by the week's total hours and half of that extra rate is paid for each overtime hour: $200 in the 48-hour example adds $16.67. Use the hours calculator to total shifts and breaks first, and the percentage calculator for a raise. For a yearly, monthly or hourly figure and take-home pay, use the salary calculator.

    Common questions

    How do you calculate overtime pay?

    Multiply your hourly rate by the overtime multiplier (1.5 for time and a half) and by the number of overtime hours, then add your regular pay. At $22.00 an hour and 48 hours in a week: 40 × $22.00 = $880.00 regular, plus 8 × $33.00 = $264.00 overtime, for a total of $1,144.00.

    What is time and a half, and what is double time?

    Time and a half means overtime hours pay 1.5 times your regular hourly rate ($22.00 becomes $33.00); double time pays 2 times ($44.00). Federal law requires time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek for covered, non-exempt employees; double time comes from state law (such as California), a contract or an employer policy. Choose the multiplier at the top of the calculator, or use the 1.5, 2 and 2.5 buttons.

    How is overtime calculated in California?

    California counts overtime by the day as well as the week: 1.5 times your regular rate after 8 hours in a day (up to 12), 2 times after 12 hours, and on the seventh consecutive day of the workweek 1.5 times for the first 8 hours and 2 times after that. Hours over 40 regular hours in a week are paid at 1.5 times as well. For 9, 10, 8, 13, 8, 0, 0 hours Monday to Friday at $22.00, the calculator's California rule gives 40 regular, 7 overtime and 1 double-time hours ($1,155.00), while the federal weekly rule gives 40 regular and 8 overtime hours ($1,144.00). Source: California Labor Commissioner's Office.

    Is overtime taxed more than regular pay?

    No. Overtime is taxed like the rest of your wages. It can look like a bigger bite because payroll withholds as if every paycheck were as large as that week's, so a big overtime check is withheld at a higher rate than usual — but the tax you actually owe for the year depends on your total income, and any over-withholding comes back as a refund. In the example, $264.00 of overtime pay leaves you about $212.12 after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare (no state tax).

    What is the no tax on overtime deduction?

    For tax years 2025 through 2028 you can deduct the overtime premium that federal law requires — generally the "half" in time and a half — up to $12,500 a year ($25,000 if you file jointly). The deduction is reduced by $100 for every $1,000 your modified adjusted gross income is over $150,000 ($300,000 joint), and it is available whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. Married people must file jointly. In the example, 8 overtime hours a week at $22.00 is a $88.00 premium a week, $4,576 over a year, and about $549 less federal income tax. Sources: IRS and 26 U.S.C. § 225.

    Does the no tax on overtime deduction cover Social Security, Medicare and state tax?

    No. It lowers federal income tax only, so Social Security and Medicare tax are still taken from the full overtime check. The calculator shows the federal saving and does not change state tax, because states treat the deduction differently — check your state's rules.

    Is a bonus included when overtime is calculated?

    Yes, a non-discretionary bonus (one promised for hours, production or staying) is part of your regular rate, so overtime is owed on it too. The federal method divides the bonus by the total hours worked in the week and pays half of that extra rate for each overtime hour. A $200 bonus in the 48-hour example adds $16.67 of overtime on top of the bonus itself (total pay $1,360.67). Enter the bonus under Go advanced. California computes some bonuses differently, so the bonus box is for the federal rule.

    How many hours before overtime starts?

    Under federal law overtime starts after 40 hours in a workweek for covered employees; some states and employers use a daily limit or a lower weekly one. The calculator's "Overtime starts after" box defaults to 40 and can be changed, and the day-by-day mode applies California's daily rules. A few other states have their own rules; check your state labor department. Employees classified as exempt are not entitled to overtime.
