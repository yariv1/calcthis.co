How to use the net carb calculator

Choose whether the label is US / Canada or UK / EU, then type the total carbohydrate, fiber and sugar alcohol in grams. If there is sugar alcohol, pick which one. The result shows the net carbs, a strict count (all sugar alcohol included) and a lenient count (none), and where the grams go. Press Add to my day to keep a running total against your daily limit.

The net carbs formula

net carbs = total carbohydrate − fiber − sugar alcohol × (1 − counted share) US / CANADA LABEL · ON A UK / EU LABEL THE CARBOHYDRATE ALREADY EXCLUDES FIBRE

US label with 22 g total carbohydrate, 6 g fiber and 8 g maltitol (counted at 35%): 22 − 6 − 8 × 0.65 = 10.8 g. Strict is 16 g, lenient 8 g. On a UK label the same food reads 16 g carbohydrate and 6 g fibre: 16 − 8 × 0.65 = 10.8 g.

Why three counts

Net carbs is not an official label figure, and fiber and sugar alcohols are not absorbed equally. Strict subtracts only the fiber, lenient subtracts the fiber and all the sugar alcohol, and the best estimate subtracts the fiber and the share of the sugar alcohol that is not counted. If a food fits your limit even by the strict count, it fits by every count.

Net carbs and your daily limit

On a ketogenic diet the usual range is 20–50 g of carbohydrate a day. Pick 20, 25, 30 or 50 g (or your own under Go advanced) and the tally under the result shows what is left. The keto calculator turns that limit into calories, protein and fat.

Common questions

What are net carbs?

Net carbs are the carbohydrates that raise blood sugar: total carbohydrate minus the fiber and, in part, the sugar alcohols, which are digested little or not at all. It is a counting convention rather than a line on a nutrition label, so different tools subtract different amounts — this calculator shows three counts so you can see the spread.

How do I calculate net carbs?

On a US or Canadian label: net carbs = total carbohydrate − fiber − the part of the sugar alcohol that does not count. For 22 g total carbohydrate, 6 g fiber and 8 g maltitol: 22 − 6 − (8 × (1 − 0.35)) = 10.8 g. Counting all the sugar alcohol (strict) gives 16 g; counting none of it (lenient) gives 8 g.

Do sugar alcohols count as carbs?

Partly, and it depends on which one. The calculator counts each by its glycaemic index relative to glucose (Livesey, 2003): erythritol 0, xylitol 13, sorbitol 9 and maltitol 35 — so 0%, 13%, 9% and 35% of the grams. For “other, mixed or not sure” it counts half, the common rule of thumb. Maltitol is the one that most often surprises people.

Why do US and EU labels give different numbers?

They define carbohydrate differently. US and Canadian labels show Total Carbohydrate, which includes dietary fiber and sugar alcohol, so you subtract the fiber. UK and EU labels show Carbohydrate without the fibre (fibre is listed separately), with sugar alcohols (polyols) inside it, so you do not subtract fibre again. A UK label saying 16 g carbohydrate, 6 g fibre and 8 g maltitol gives 10.8 g net carbs — the same food as the 22 g US label above.

Should I count net carbs or total carbs on keto?

The 20–50 g a day range in the ketogenic-diet research is stated as carbohydrate, so total carbs is the strictest reading. Counting net carbs lets more fibre-rich vegetables fit and is what most keto calculators use. If you are unsure, watch the strict figure: if it stays inside your limit, every method does.

How many net carbs per day?

On a ketogenic diet the usual range is 20–50 g of carbohydrate a day, varying from person to person. The day tally checks what you add against 20, 25, 30 or 50 g or your own limit. The keto calculator works out the rest of your macros from it.

Can I rely on net carbs if I have diabetes?

Be careful. Net carb counts can understate how much a food raises blood sugar, because fiber and sugar alcohols differ in how much they are absorbed. If you take insulin or other diabetes medication, ask your care team how to count carbohydrate for dosing rather than using net carbs.

What does the “per 100 g” option do?

Many labels give values per 100 g as well as per serving. Choose Per 100 g, enter the figures from that column, and type how many grams you ate — the calculator scales the result. Leave the amount empty to see the net carbs in 100 g.

How does the day tally work?

After each food, press Add to my day. The list adds up your net carbs (best estimate), shows how many grams are left of your daily limit and what the strict and lenient totals would be, so one mislabelled bar does not blow the day.
