How to use the recipe converter

Paste your ingredients into the box, one per line, in any common format — 2 cups flour, 1 1/2 tbsp butter, 200g sugar, butter – 250g. Type how many servings the recipe makes and how many you want, or switch to By multiplier and type 2, 3 or 1/2. Every amount is scaled and shown the way you would measure it. Pick US or Metric under Units if you want the whole recipe in one system, or leave it on As written to keep your units.

How to scale a recipe

new amount = old amount × (servings wanted ÷ servings in the recipe) THE SAME FACTOR GOES ON EVERY INGREDIENT

A recipe for 4 that you need for 6 has a factor of 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Multiply every ingredient by it. The hard part is not the multiplication — it is that the answer is rarely something you can measure. Here is that recipe at 1.5×:

IngredientOriginalAt 1.5×
All-purpose flour2 cups3 cups
Sugar½ cup¾ cup
Large eggs35 (exactly 4½)
Vanilla extract1 tsp1½ tsp
Butter1½ tbsp2 tbsp + 1 tsp (exactly 2¼ tbsp)

The converter shows the exact value next to anything it had to round, so you always know how far the kitchen-friendly amount is from the maths.

Scaling by pan size

When a recipe is written for one pan and you only have another, scale by area, not by the length of the sides. Open Go advanced, choose Pan size, and enter both pans. An 8 × 8 inch pan holds 64 square inches and a 9 × 13 inch pan holds 117, so the 9 × 13 needs 1.83× the recipe. A 9-inch round pan (63.6 square inches) to an 8-inch round pan (50.3) is 0.79×. Scaling by area keeps the batter at the same depth, so the baking time stays close to the original.

What doesn’t scale in a straight line

  • Salt and strong spices — past about 3× add them gradually and taste. The converter flags these lines.
  • Baking powder, baking soda and yeast — fine for doubling or halving, but unreliable at much larger or smaller factors.
  • Eggs — you can’t use 4½ eggs, so round to a whole egg or beat one and measure part of it (a large egg is about 3 tablespoons).
  • Time and heat — these depend on the depth of food in the pan, not the amount. Check for doneness early.

Cups to grams

Common questions

How do I scale a recipe up or down?

Divide the servings you want by the servings the recipe makes to get a scale factor, then multiply every ingredient by it. A recipe for 4 that you need for 6 has a factor of 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5, so 2 cups of flour becomes 3 cups. This converter does the multiplication for every line and rounds each result to an amount you can measure.

How many cups is 250 grams of flour?

About 2 cups plus 1½ tablespoons. All-purpose flour weighs roughly 120 grams per US cup, so 250 g is 250 ÷ 120 = 2.08 cups. The exact figure depends on how the flour is scooped, which is why weighing is more accurate than cups for baking.

What do I do when a scaled recipe needs part of an egg?

Round to the nearest whole egg, or beat one egg and measure out the part you need — a large egg is about 3 tablespoons, so half an egg is about 1½ tablespoons. The converter rounds to a whole egg and shows the exact figure, for example 4½ eggs, so you can choose.

How do I convert a recipe from US cups to metric?

Choose Metric under Units. Dry and solid ingredients become grams using a per-ingredient density (1 cup of all-purpose flour is 120 g, 1 cup of granulated sugar 200 g, 1 cup of butter 227 g) and liquids become millilitres (1 US cup is about 240 ml). Ingredients the converter doesn’t know are converted by volume only.

Should I change the baking time when I scale a recipe?

Cooking time depends on depth, not on the amount. If you scale by pan area, the batter keeps the same depth and the time should stay close to the original. If you make a bigger batch in the same pan it will be deeper and need longer; a smaller batch in the same pan is shallower and cooks sooner. Check for doneness a few minutes early either way.
