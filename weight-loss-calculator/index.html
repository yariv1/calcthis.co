How to use the weight loss calculator

Pick female or male, enter your age, height, activity level, current weight and goal weight — or, next to Calories you burn, choose I know it and type the calories you burn a day (your maintenance). Then pick a target date (or tap 4 weeks, 12 weeks, 6 months), or switch to a weekly pace to see the date it gives.

You get the calories to eat a day, the loss per week as a share of your body weight, the fastest date within the guidelines, a weight curve, milestone dates and the calories for six other target dates. Open Go advanced to choose the realistic estimate as the headline or to enter your body fat.

The weight loss by date formula

Calories a day = maintenance − (weight to lose × 7,700 ÷ days) MAINTENANCE = BMR × ACTIVITY FACTOR · 7,700 KCAL PER KG (3,500 PER LB)

For a woman of 35, 168 cm and 80 kg with light activity: BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor) = 1,514 kcal, maintenance = 1,514 × 1.375 = 2,082 kcal. To lose 10 kg in 140 days she needs a deficit of 10 × 7,700 ÷ 140 = 550 kcal a day, so she eats about 1,532 kcal and loses 0.50 kg (1.10 lb) a week.

Safe pace and minimums

  • 1 to 2 pounds a week. The CDC links a gradual, steady pace of about 1 to 2 pounds (0.45–0.9 kg) a week with keeping the weight off.
  • 5–10% over about 6 months. NHLBI’s recommendation for people with overweight or obesity.
  • A floor on what you eat. The calculator flags under 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 for men, the commonly used minimums.
  • Under 18, pregnant or with a medical condition: talk to a doctor before cutting calories.

Simple rule or realistic?

The simple rule spreads 7,700 kcal per kg evenly. In real life you burn about 14 kcal less per kg lost, so the same intake gives a smaller deficit as you get lighter. To arrive on the same date the realistic plan starts lower — 1,460 kcal instead of 1,532 in the example — and rises as you lose. The weight curve plots both. See also the calorie deficit calculator.

Common questions

How do I calculate weight loss by a date?

Work out the weight to lose, turn it into calories, and spread it over the days you have. For the example (80 kg to 70 kg in 20 weeks): 10 kg × 7,700 kcal ÷ 140 days = 550 kcal a day. Your maintenance is 2,082 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor 1,514 × 1.375), so you eat 2,082 − 550 = 1,532 kcal. The calculator does this for your numbers and checks it against the minimum and pace limits.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight by a date?

Your maintenance minus the daily deficit the date needs. In the example the date needs 550 kcal a day, so about 1,532 kcal. A closer date needs a bigger deficit: the same goal in 8 weeks would need 1,375 kcal a day, leaving 707 kcal, which the calculator flags as too fast.

How fast can I lose weight safely?

The CDC says people who lose weight at a gradual, steady pace — about 1 to 2 pounds (0.45–0.9 kg) a week — are more likely to keep it off, and NHLBI recommends 5% to 10% of initial weight over about 6 months for people with overweight or obesity. The calculator flags a plan faster than 2 lb a week or 1% of your body weight a week, or one that puts you under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men).

What if my target date is too soon?

The calculator still shows the calories the date would need, flags them, and gives the fastest date within the guidelines — for the example, 13 weeks eating 1,202 kcal. If the date would need more calories than you burn, it says so and shows that date instead of a blank result.

Why are there two numbers, simple and realistic?

The simple rule treats your body as a machine that burns the same every day: 7,700 kcal per kg, spread evenly. In real life you burn about 14 kcal less a day for every kg you lose, so the same intake becomes a smaller deficit. To still arrive on the date you have to start lower: 1,460 kcal at first instead of 1,532 for the example, rising as you lose. The calculator shows both and lets you choose the headline under Go advanced.

How many calories is 1 kg or 1 lb of weight loss?

About 7,700 kcal per kg, or 3,500 per lb, is the usual rule of thumb used here as the simple estimate. It is an approximation: the real mix of fat, muscle and water changes with the person, and your body burns less as you lose weight, which is why the realistic estimate is shown too.

How much weight can I lose in a month?

At the pace of 0.5 kg (1.1 lb) a week it is about 2.2 kg (4.8 lb) a month, which needs a deficit of 550 kcal a day. At 1 kg (2.2 lb) a week it is about 4.3 kg a month and 1,100 kcal a day — more than the CDC’s 1 to 2 pounds a week for most people. Use “At a pace” to see the date a pace gives.

What percentage of my body weight is this?

The calculator shows it for you: 10 kg of 80 kg is 12.5% of the example’s body weight, which is in the 5–10% range NHLBI gives for about 6 months. Our percentage change calculator works out any other before-and-after figure.

Is this the same as the calorie deficit calculator?

They use the same energy model but start from different questions. This page starts from a goal weight and a date and gives you the calories. The calorie deficit calculator starts from a deficit, a pace or what you eat, and the calorie calculator gives a pace table with a protein target.

Can I use it to gain weight?

No — it plans a loss. For a calorie surplus use the bulking calculator.
