What is BMR?

Your basal metabolic rate is the energy your body burns at complete rest — breathing, pumping blood, keeping warm, repairing cells. It is usually the biggest part of the calories you burn in a day, roughly 60–70% for most people.

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula

BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women) THE MOST WIDELY RECOMMENDED EQUATION FOR ADULTS

A 30-year-old woman who is 165 cm and 65 kg: 10 × 65 = 650, plus 6.25 × 165 = 1,031.25, minus 5 × 30 = 150, minus 161 = 1,370 kcal a day. The same figure in kilojoules is 1,370 × 4.184 = 5,733 kJ, or 57 kcal an hour.

Harris-Benedict1919The original; tends to read highest
Schofield1985WHO/FAO; the only one for ages under 18
Katch-McArdle1975Uses lean mass, so needs your body fat %

They were built from different groups of people decades apart, so they routinely differ by 50–150 kcal for the same person. That spread is the honest uncertainty in any estimate.

BMR, RMR and TDEE

BMR is what you burn at rest. RMR is measured under looser conditions and reads a little higher (5–10%); in practice the same equations are used for both. TDEE — total daily energy expenditure — is BMR plus digestion and movement, which is the number that decides whether you gain or lose weight.

What changes your BMR?

  • Lean mass. Muscle burns more than fat at rest, which is why Katch-McArdle uses it.
  • Body size. Bigger bodies burn more, which is why weight and height are in every formula.
  • Age. BMR drifts down with age, mostly from losing lean mass.
  • Sex. Men tend to burn more at the same weight because they carry more lean mass.
  • Hormones, illness and genetics. Thyroid problems, pregnancy and your own biology can move it either way — a formula can't see these.

Common questions

What is BMR and how is it calculated?

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the energy your body burns at complete rest to keep you alive — breathing, circulation, temperature and cell repair. The most widely used equation is Mifflin-St Jeor: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age, then + 5 for men or − 161 for women. A 30-year-old woman who is 165 cm and 65 kg has a BMR of about 1,370 kcal a day.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is what you burn at rest. TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) is what you burn in a whole day, which adds digestion and movement. You get TDEE by multiplying BMR by an activity factor from 1.2 for a sedentary day to 1.9 for hard training or a physical job.

Which BMR formula is the most accurate?

Mifflin-St Jeor is generally the best of the common equations for most adults, and it is the headline formula here. Katch-McArdle can be more accurate if you know your body fat percentage, especially for lean or muscular people. The formulas often differ by 50 to 150 kcal for the same person, so treat any result as a range of roughly 10% either side.

Should I eat less than my BMR to lose weight?

Not for long. Your BMR is the energy your body needs at rest, so eating well below it for weeks makes it harder to get enough nutrients and to keep muscle. A deficit is normally taken from your TDEE instead, for example 500 kcal below it for about half a kilo (1 lb) a week. If you are considering a very low intake, talk to a doctor or dietitian first.

Is BMR the same as RMR?

They are very close. BMR is measured fasted, fully rested and warm; RMR (resting metabolic rate) is measured under looser conditions and usually reads 5 to 10% higher. The prediction equations are built from measurements of both and are used for either in practice.
