    North Carolina sales tax at a glance

    North Carolina’s state rate is 4.75%. Every county adds a local tax and four add a 0.5% transit tax, and the rate that applies is the rate of the county where the sale is made. In the Department of Revenue’s table effective July 1, 2026 the combined rate runs from 6.75% to 8.25% (Mecklenburg County, which added 1% on July 1, 2026).

    Qualifying food is taxed differently: a flat 2% local rate and no state, transit or other local tax, so groceries cost the same in every county. Wake County (Raleigh) is 7.25%, Durham 7.5%, Guilford (Greensboro) 6.75% and Mecklenburg (Charlotte) 8.25%.

    What is taxed and what is exempt

    Item typeSales taxDetails
    Most other goods, including clothingTaxableTaxable at your county’s full rate. Over-the-counter medicine, candy, soft drinks, dietary supplements and alcohol are taxable at the full rate too.
    Qualifying food (groceries)TaxableQualifying food pays only a 2% local rate: the state, transit and other local rates do not apply. It is all food except candy, soft drinks, prepared food, dietary supplements and vending-machine food.
    Prepared food and restaurant mealsTaxableFood sold hot or heated by the seller, or sold with the seller’s eating utensils, is prepared food and pays the full county rate. Some counties add a separate local prepared-meal tax that is not included here.
    Prescription drugs and insulinExemptDrugs that federal law lets only a prescription dispense, over-the-counter drugs sold on a prescription, and insulin are exempt. Other over-the-counter medicine is taxable.

    Shipping and delivery

    Under the Department’s taxability matrix a transportation or shipping charge listed separately on the invoice is not taxed, while handling, crating and packing are taxable. If the charge is combined with handling or not listed separately, the whole charge is taxed — tick the box in the calculator. On a $100 item in Wake County with $10 shipping the tax is $7.25 when the shipping is listed separately and $7.98 when it is billed with handling.

    Local taxes, special districts and fees

    • County rate on top of the 4.75% state tax. Every county adds its own local tax to the 4.75% state rate and four add a 0.5% transit tax, so the combined rate in the July 1, 2026 table runs from 6.75% to 8.25%. Search your city or county above.
    • Mecklenburg County (Charlotte). Since July 1, 2026 Mecklenburg levies an additional 1% local tax: 8.25% in total, up from 7.25%. The additional 1% does not apply to qualifying food (2%), aircraft, electricity, boats, manufactured and modular homes, telecommunications, video programming and a few other items taxed at their own rate.
    • Transit counties. Durham, Orange and Wake counties (7.5%, 7.5% and 7.25%) and Mecklenburg include the 0.5% transit tax. The rates here already contain it.
    • Groceries at 2%. Qualifying food is taxed at a 2% local rate only. Candy, soft drinks, prepared food, dietary supplements, vending-machine food and alcohol are not qualifying food and pay the full county rate.
    • Prepared-meal taxes. The Department’s rates do not include prepared-meal (restaurant) taxes some local governments impose and administer themselves, so a restaurant bill can cost more than the rate shown.
    • Medicine. Prescription drugs, over-the-counter drugs sold on a prescription and insulin are exempt. Other over-the-counter medicine is taxable.
    • Delivery charges. A transportation or shipping charge listed separately on the invoice is not taxed; handling, crating and packing charges are, and so is a charge that is combined or not listed separately. The calculator shows both cases with the shipping box.
    • Bought online or out of state. If the seller did not charge North Carolina tax you owe use tax at the rate of the county where you use the item. Pick your county and enter any tax the seller charged under Go advanced.
    • Rates change on July 1 and January 1. Local rates change only when a county acts; Mecklenburg’s 1% increase took effect on July 1, 2026. The rates here are from the table effective July 1, 2026.

    Common questions about North Carolina sales tax

    What is the sales tax rate in North Carolina?

    North Carolina’s state rate is 4.75% and every county adds its own local tax, so the combined rate in the Department of Revenue’s table effective July 1, 2026 runs from 6.75% to 8.25%. Wake County (Raleigh) is 7.25%, Durham 7.5%, Guilford (Greensboro) 6.75%, Forsyth (Winston-Salem) 7% and Mecklenburg (Charlotte) 8.25%. Search your city or county in the calculator above.

    Why did the sales tax in Charlotte go up to 8.25%?

    Mecklenburg County levied an additional 1% local sales and use tax effective July 1, 2026, taking the combined rate from 7.25% to 8.25%. A $100 purchase now costs $108.25 instead of $107.25. The additional 1% does not apply to qualifying food, which stays at 2%.

    Is food taxed in North Carolina?

    Qualifying food pays only a 2% local rate — the state, transit and other local rates do not apply — so a $100 grocery bill has $2.00 of tax in every county, Charlotte included. Candy, soft drinks, prepared food, dietary supplements, vending-machine food and alcohol are not qualifying food and pay the full county rate: $7.25 on $100 of restaurant food in Wake County. Choose the item type in the calculator to see the difference.

    Is clothing taxed in North Carolina?

    The Department of Revenue pages we used list no clothing exemption, so clothing is calculated at your county’s full rate — $7.25 on $100 in Wake County.

    Are prescription drugs taxed in North Carolina?

    No. Drugs that federal law lets only a prescription dispense, over-the-counter drugs sold on a prescription, and insulin are exempt. Other over-the-counter medicine is taxable. Choose “Prescription” as the item type to see the tax removed.

    Is shipping taxed in North Carolina?

    Under the Department’s taxability matrix a transportation or shipping charge listed separately on the invoice is not taxed, while handling, crating and packing are taxable, and a charge that is combined or not listed separately is taxed with the sale. On a $100 item in Wake County with $10 shipping the tax is $7.25 when the shipping is listed separately and $7.98 when it is billed with handling. Tick the shipping box in the calculator for the second case.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total in North Carolina?

    Divide the total by 1 plus your county’s rate. In Wake County (7.25%) a total of $107.25 is $100.00 before tax, with $7.25 of tax inside it. Switch the calculator to “Remove tax”, pick your county and enter the total.

    Do I owe North Carolina tax on things I buy online?

    Yes, when the seller does not charge North Carolina tax you owe use tax at the rate of the county where you use the item. A $3,000 item used in Wake County with no tax charged means $217.50 of use tax; if the seller charged 4% you still owe $97.50. Enter the tax the seller charged under Go advanced.

    Do restaurants charge more than the North Carolina sales tax rate?

    Sometimes. The Department’s rates do not include prepared-meal taxes that some local governments impose and administer themselves, so check the receipt for a separate prepared-meal tax.
