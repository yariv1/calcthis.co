    Chicago sales tax at a glance

    The Illinois Department of Revenue’s rate file lists Chicago at 10.5% on general merchandise from August 1, 2026 (it was 10.25% until July 31, 2026). On $100 that is $10.50 of tax, for a total of $110.50.

    Chicago does not tax everything at the full rate. Groceries pay 1.5% and drugs and medical items 2.5%, while candy, soft drinks, alcohol and restaurant food pay the full rate. Many websites still show 10.25%.

    Shipping and delivery

    In Illinois a delivery charge is part of the price when it is inseparable from the sale — for example when it is not listed separately on the bill. A delivery charge that is listed separately for a delivery the buyer chose is generally not taxed. Tick the box in the calculator when the charge is not listed separately; ask the store if you are unsure. On a $100 item with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $10.50; when the shipping is not listed separately it is $11.55.

    Other taxes and special rates

    • One rate across the city. The Illinois Department of Revenue lists Chicago at 10.5% on general merchandise since August 1, 2026 (10.25% until July 31, 2026). A store on the edge of the city or in a special taxing district can charge another rate; the Department’s MyTax Illinois Tax Rate Finder gives the rate for one address.
    • Groceries are taxed in Chicago. Illinois ended its 1% state tax on groceries on January 1, 2026, but Chicago keeps a local grocery tax: the Department’s grocery file lists 1.5% since August 1, 2026 (1.25% before). Candy, soft drinks, alcohol and prepared food pay the full rate.
    • Medicine. Illinois taxes drugs and medical items at a lower rate than general merchandise: 2.5% in Chicago.
    • Chicago’s own taxes. Chicago also charges its own restaurant and beverage taxes, which are not part of the sales tax rate used here. Check the City of Chicago Department of Finance for those.
    • Delivery charges. A delivery charge is taxed when it is inseparable from the sale, for example when it is not listed separately on the bill. A separately listed charge for a delivery the buyer chose is generally not taxed.
    • Cars. Vehicles that must be titled or registered follow their own rules and rates. The calculator does not compute them.
    • Bought online or out of state. If the seller did not charge Illinois tax you owe use tax at the rate where you use the item. Enter the rate the seller charged under Go advanced.

    Common questions about Chicago sales tax

    What is the sales tax rate in Chicago?

    10.5% on most goods since August 1, 2026, according to the Illinois Department of Revenue’s rate file; it was 10.25% until July 31, 2026. On a $100 purchase the tax is $10.50 and the total is $110.50; on $1,000 it is $105.00, for $1,105.00.

    Is the Chicago sales tax 10.25% or 10.5%?

    It is 10.5% for sales from August 1, 2026. The Illinois Department of Revenue file shows 10.25% as the rate that ended on July 31, 2026, and many websites have not updated yet. A store in a special taxing district can charge another rate; the department’s MyTax Illinois Tax Rate Finder gives the exact rate for one address.

    Are groceries taxed in Chicago?

    Yes, at a lower rate. Illinois ended its 1% state tax on groceries on January 1, 2026, but Chicago keeps a local grocery tax: the department’s grocery file lists 1.5% from August 1, 2026 (1.25% before). A $100 grocery bill has $1.50 of tax. Candy, soft drinks, alcohol and food prepared for immediate consumption are not groceries and pay the full 10.5%: $10.50 on $100 of candy and soda, $4.20 on a $40 restaurant meal.

    What is the sales tax on medicine in Chicago?

    Illinois taxes drugs and medical items at a lower “Drug & Medical” rate than general merchandise: 2.5% in Chicago, or $2.50 on $100.

    Is clothing taxed in Chicago?

    Yes. Illinois has no clothing exemption, so clothing is taxed at the full 10.5% — $10.50 on $100.

    Is shipping taxed in Chicago?

    A delivery charge is part of the price when it is inseparable from the sale, for example when it is not listed separately on the bill; a delivery charge listed separately for a delivery the buyer chose is generally not taxed. On a $100 item with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $10.50; when the shipping is not listed separately it is $11.55. Tick the box in the calculator for the second case and ask the store if you are unsure.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total in Chicago?

    Divide the total by 1.105. A total of $110.50 is $100.00 before tax, with $10.50 of tax inside it. Switch the calculator to “Remove tax” to do it for any amount.

    Do I owe tax on things I buy online?

    Yes, when the seller does not charge Illinois tax you owe use tax at the rate where you use the item. A $3,000 item used in Chicago with no tax charged means $315.00 of use tax; if the seller charged 4% you still owe $195.00. Enter the rate the seller charged under Go advanced.

    Does Chicago charge any other taxes?

    Chicago also charges its own restaurant and beverage taxes, which are not part of the sales tax rate used here. Check the City of Chicago Department of Finance for those.
