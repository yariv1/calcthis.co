    Missouri sales tax at a glance

    Missouri’s state rate is 4.225%. Cities, counties and special districts add their own tax, and the seller collects the combined rate for the store’s location. In the October–December 2026 table the combined rate runs from 5.225% to 11.488% by city and county, and up to 13.238% inside some Community Improvement Districts and Transportation Development Districts. Local rates change in January, April, July and October.

    Even one city can have several rates — Kansas City is 8.725% in Clay County, 8.975% in Jackson County, 8.725% in Platte County and 9.1% in Cass County — so the calculator looks up your exact place instead of guessing.

    Shipping and delivery

    A delivery charge that is usual, customary and listed separately on the bill is not taxed. If shipping and handling are billed as one charge, the whole charge is taxed — use the shipping box in the calculator. On a $100 item in Springfield with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $8.10; billed as one shipping-and-handling charge it is $8.91.

    Local taxes, special districts and fees

    • Local tax everywhere. Every place in Missouri adds city, county or district tax to the 4.225% state rate. In the October–December 2026 table the combined rate runs from 5.225% to 11.488%, and up to 13.238% inside some special districts. Search your place above.
    • Shopping-center districts (CID, TDD). A Community Improvement District or Transportation Development District adds its own tax inside its boundaries. Each one is a separate line in the place search — pick it if the store is inside one.
    • Groceries. The state rate on food for home use is 1.225% (down from 4.225%). Local taxes still apply to food and drink, and a few places leave some of them off food — the calculator uses the food rate listed for your place.
    • Restaurants and amusement places. The reduced food rate does not apply when more than 80% of a seller’s gross receipts are prepared food, or to food sold at places of amusement, entertainment or recreation. Food trucks charge the full rate of the place where they sell.
    • Delivery charges. Separately stated, usual and customary delivery charges are not taxed (RSMo 144.010; DOR letter ruling 8387). A combined “shipping and handling” charge is taxed in full.
    • Back-to-school sales tax holiday. From 12:01 a.m. on the first Friday in August until midnight on Sunday, clothing of $100 or less, school supplies up to $50, software up to $350, computers and peripherals up to $1,500 and graphing calculators up to $150 are free of state and local sales tax. The calculator does not apply the holiday — outside those days all of these are taxed at your place’s rate.
    • Adult-use marijuana. Some places add a separate local tax on adult-use marijuana. It is not included in this calculator.
    • Bought online or out of state. If the seller did not charge Missouri tax you owe use tax at the state rate of 4.225% plus the local use tax where you use the item. Pick your place and enter any tax the seller charged under Go advanced. A use tax return is required once your untaxed purchases pass $2,000 in a year.
    • Rates change every quarter. Local rates change in January, April, July and October. The rates here are from the October–December 2026 table.

    Common questions about Missouri sales tax

    What is the sales tax rate in Missouri?

    Missouri’s state sales tax rate is 4.225%, and every city, county and district adds its own tax on top. In the October–December 2026 rate table the combined rate runs from 5.225% to 11.488% by city and county, and up to 13.238% inside some shopping-center districts. Springfield is 8.1%, Columbia 7.975% and St. Louis city 9.679%. Search your place in the calculator above for your exact rate.

    Why is Missouri sales tax different in every city?

    Cities, counties and special districts can each add a local sales tax to the 4.225% state rate, and the seller collects the combined rate for where the store is. Even one city can have several rates: Kansas City is 8.725% in Clay County, 8.975% in Jackson County, 8.725% in Platte County and 9.1% in Cass County. A $100 purchase costs $108.10 in Springfield but $109.68 in St. Louis city.

    Is food taxed in Missouri?

    Yes, but at a lower rate. The state rate on food for home use is cut to 1.225%, and local taxes still apply, so groceries are taxed at the “food” rate listed for your place: 5.1% in Springfield ($5.10 on $100) and 6.391% in St. Louis city ($6.39 on $100). Restaurant food is taxed at the full rate, and so is all food at a seller whose prepared food is more than 80% of its sales.

    Is clothing taxed in Missouri?

    Yes. Clothing is taxed at the full combined rate for your place — $9.68 on $100 in St. Louis city. The exception is the back-to-school sales tax holiday, from 12:01 a.m. on the first Friday in August through Sunday, when clothing of $100 or less is free of state and local sales tax.

    Are prescription drugs taxed in Missouri?

    No. Prescription drugs, insulin and hearing aids are exempt. Over-the-counter medicine is taxable unless it is prescribed or bought by a person with a disability. Choose “Prescription” as the item type to see the tax removed.

    Is shipping taxed in Missouri?

    Not when the delivery charge is usual, customary and listed separately on the bill (RSMo 144.010; DOR letter ruling 8387, March 24, 2026). A combined “shipping and handling” charge is taxed in full. On a $100 item in Springfield with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $8.10; billed as one charge it is $8.91. Tick the box in the calculator for the combined case.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total in Missouri?

    Divide the total by 1 plus your combined rate. In Springfield (8.1%) a total of $108.10 is $100.00 before tax, with $8.10 of tax inside it. Switch the calculator to “Remove tax”, pick your place and enter the total.

    Do I owe Missouri tax on things I buy online?

    Yes, when the seller does not charge Missouri tax you owe use tax at the state rate of 4.225% plus the local use tax where you use the item. A $3,000 item bought for use in Springfield with no tax charged means $190.50 of use tax; if the seller charged 4% you still owe $70.50. A use tax return is required once your untaxed purchases pass $2,000 in a year. Enter the tax the seller charged under Go advanced.
