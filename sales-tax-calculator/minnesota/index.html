    Minnesota sales tax at a glance

    Minnesota’s state rate is 6.875%. Local taxes are added on top: a county or transit tax (often 0.5%), a city tax in some cities, and 1% for housing and transportation in the seven Twin Cities metro counties. In the Department of Revenue’s October–December 2026 guide the combined rate runs from 6.875% to 9.875% (St. Paul). Local rates change in January, April, July and October.

    Groceries, general clothing and medicine are exempt, so only part of a typical shopping trip is taxed. Minneapolis is 9.025%, St. Paul 9.875%, Rochester 8.125% and Duluth 8.875% — so the calculator looks up your exact place instead of guessing.

    What is taxed and what is exempt

    Item typeSales taxDetails
    Most other goodsTaxableMost goods are taxable. Vitamins and other dietary supplements, candy, soft drinks, alcohol and tobacco products (but not cigarettes) are taxable too.
    Groceries (food for home)ExemptFood and food ingredients are exempt. Candy, soft drinks, alcohol, prepared food, dietary supplements and food from vending machines are taxable.
    Clothing and shoesExemptGeneral-use clothing is exempt at any price. Fur clothing, clothing accessories (handbags, jewelry, nonprescription sunglasses), sports or recreational equipment and protective equipment are taxable.
    Prescription and over-the-counter drugsExemptPrescription drugs and over-the-counter drugs (with a Drug Facts panel or active-ingredient list) are exempt. Vitamins and minerals are taxable dietary supplements.
    Restaurant and prepared foodTaxableFood prepared by the seller or sold with eating utensils is taxable. Some cities add a food and beverage tax on top — see the note under your place.
    Candy and soft drinksTaxableTaxable, even in a grocery store. Soft drinks include sweetened water and fruit drinks with 50% juice or less.

    Shipping and delivery

    A delivery charge from the seller is taxed when the item is taxable and not taxed when the item is exempt. A mixed shipment is split by price or weight, and a carrier you pay directly is not taxed. On a $100 taxable item in Minneapolis with $10 shipping the tax is $9.93; on $100 of exempt groceries with $10 shipping it is $0.00.

    Local taxes, special districts and fees

    • Local tax depends on the place. Minnesota’s state rate is 6.875%. Cities, counties and transit areas add their own, so in the October–December 2026 guide the combined rate runs from 6.875% to 9.875% (St. Paul). Search your place above.
    • Twin Cities metro tax. The seven metro counties — Anoka, Carver, Dakota, Hennepin, Ramsey, Scott and Washington — add 1% (0.25% for housing and 0.75% for transportation). Many counties outside the metro add a 0.5% county or transit tax.
    • Restaurant, entertainment and liquor taxes. Some places add a special tax that is not in the combined rate: a food and beverage tax on restaurant sales in Detroit Lakes (1%), Mankato (0.5%), Marshall (1.5%), North Mankato (0.5%), Proctor (1%) and the Giants Ridge area (1%); St. Cloud (1% food and 1% liquor); Mankato (0.5% entertainment); and in Minneapolis a 3% entertainment tax everywhere plus 3% restaurant and liquor taxes downtown. A 2.5% liquor gross receipts tax can also apply. Your place’s extras are shown under the search box.
    • Hotels and lodging. Short-term lodging is taxable, and many places add a local lodging tax: for example 7% in Rochester and St. Paul (hotels with 50 or more rooms), 3% in Minneapolis, Plymouth, Richfield, Woodbury and Cottage Grove, and 4.5% in Marshall. Lodging taxes are not included in the calculator.
    • Food, clothing and medicine. Groceries, general clothing, prescription drugs and over-the-counter drugs are exempt. Candy, soft drinks, alcohol, prepared food, supplements, fur, clothing accessories, sports gear and protective equipment are taxable.
    • Delivery charges. A delivery charge from the seller follows the item: taxed with a taxable item, not taxed with an exempt one. A separate retail delivery fee of 50 cents applies to qualifying deliveries of $100 or more; it is shown on the receipt, is not part of the sales tax and is not included here.
    • Bought online or out of state. If the seller did not charge Minnesota tax you owe use tax at the combined rate for where you use the item. Pick your place and enter any tax the seller charged under Go advanced.
    • Rates change every quarter. Local rates change in January, April, July and October. The rates here are from the October–December 2026 guide.

    Common questions about Minnesota sales tax

    What is the sales tax rate in Minnesota?

    Minnesota’s state sales tax rate is 6.875%, and cities, counties and transit areas add their own. In the Department of Revenue’s October–December 2026 guide the combined rate runs from 6.875% (no local tax) to 9.875% in St. Paul. Minneapolis is 9.025%, Rochester 8.125%, Duluth 8.875% and Owatonna 7.375%. Search your city in the calculator above for your exact rate.

    Why is Minnesota sales tax different in every city?

    The 6.875% state rate is topped up by a county or transit tax (often 0.5%), a city tax in some cities and, in the seven Twin Cities metro counties, 1% for housing and transportation. A $100 purchase costs $109.03 in Minneapolis, $109.88 in St. Paul and $108.13 in Rochester. Golden Valley is 9.775%, the highest rate in the metro outside St. Paul. Rates can change on January 1, April 1, July 1 or October 1.

    Is food taxed in Minnesota?

    Groceries are exempt: a $100 grocery bill has $0.00 of tax. Candy, soft drinks, alcohol, prepared food, dietary supplements and food from vending machines are taxable, so a $100 restaurant meal in Minneapolis has $9.03 of tax. Some places add a food and beverage tax on restaurants on top of that — Minneapolis downtown, Mankato, Marshall, North Mankato, Detroit Lakes, Proctor, St. Cloud and the Giants Ridge area — and the calculator lists your place’s extras under the search box.

    Is clothing taxed in Minnesota?

    No. General-use clothing is exempt at any price: $0.00 of tax on $100 of clothing. The exemption does not cover fur clothing, clothing accessories such as handbags, jewelry and nonprescription sunglasses, sports or recreational equipment, or protective equipment — those are taxed at your place’s rate ($9.03 on $100 in Minneapolis).

    Are prescription drugs taxed in Minnesota?

    No. Prescription drugs and over-the-counter drugs with a Drug Facts panel or active-ingredient list are exempt. Vitamins, minerals and other dietary supplements are taxable. Choose “Medicine” as the item type to see the tax removed.

    Is shipping taxed in Minnesota?

    A delivery charge from the seller follows the item: taxed with a taxable item, not taxed with an exempt one. On a $100 taxable item in Minneapolis with $10 shipping the tax is $9.93; on $100 of exempt groceries it is $0.00. A mixed shipment is split by price or weight, and a carrier you pay directly is not taxed. A separate 50-cent retail delivery fee can apply to qualifying deliveries of $100 or more; it is not part of the sales tax.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total in Minnesota?

    Divide the total by 1 plus your combined rate. In Minneapolis (9.025%) a total of $109.03 is $100.00 before tax, with $9.03 of tax inside it. Switch the calculator to “Remove tax”, pick your city and enter the total.

    Do I owe Minnesota tax on things I buy online?

    Yes, when the seller does not charge Minnesota tax you owe use tax at the combined rate for where you use the item. A $3,000 item used in Minneapolis with no tax charged means $270.75 of use tax; if the seller charged 4% you still owe $150.75. Enter the tax the seller charged under Go advanced.

    What if my city is not in the Department of Revenue list?

    The Department of Revenue guide lists only places that have a local sales tax, so the calculator offers “Anywhere else in Minnesota” at the 6.875% state rate ($106.88 on $100). If you are unsure, check your address in the department’s own Sales Tax Rate Calculator.
