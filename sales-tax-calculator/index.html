    How to use the sales tax calculator

    Pick your state, type the price and the total appears with the tax split out. Add as many items as you like, add shipping, and on a state with its own page choose the type of item — groceries, clothing and medicine, for example, can be exempt. Use Remove tax to find the price before tax from a total, and Find the rate to work out the rate from a price before and after tax.

    The state page shows every extra that can apply in that state — special zones, local taxes and fees — instead of leaving you to find out at the register.

    The sales tax formula

    Total = price × (1 + rate ÷ 100) REVERSE: PRICE = TOTAL ÷ (1 + RATE ÷ 100)

    At 8.25%, a $100 purchase has $8.25 of tax and costs $108.25. Going the other way, a $108.25 total is $100.00 before tax.

    State rate plus local rate

    Forty-five states charge a state sales tax and 38 allow cities, counties or districts to add their own. A state is added to the list only when its rules have been checked against official guidance. For any other state, type your own combined rate — the calculator never guesses a local rate.     Common questions

    How do I calculate sales tax?

    Multiply the price by the sales tax rate and add the result to the price. At 8.25% a $100 purchase has $8.25 of tax and costs $108.25. In New Jersey, which charges 6.625%, $100 plus tax is $106.63.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total?

    Divide the total by 1 plus the rate. A $108.25 total at 8.25% is $100.00 before tax, with $8.25 of tax inside it. Use the “Remove tax” tab and enter the total you paid.

    How do I find the sales tax rate from two prices?

    Subtract the price before tax from the price after tax, divide by the price before tax and multiply by 100. A purchase that costs $100 before tax and $108.25 after tax was taxed at 8.25%. The “Find the rate” tab does it for you and compares the result with the state’s own rate.

    Why does the total depend on where I live?

    Most states add a state rate, and many allow cities and counties to add their own. Texas, for example, charges 6.25% at the state level and allows up to 2% more, so $100 costs between $106.25 and $108.25. New Jersey has no local sales tax at all. Pick your state to see its rate and, for states with their own page, the exact rules.

    Which states have no sales tax?

    Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide sales tax. Alaska is the only one that allows local sales taxes. All other states and the District of Columbia have one.

    Is the result exact?

    On a state page the result is exact for the cases the page lists, with the source and date shown. For a state without its own page yet, type your own combined rate — the calculator never guesses a local rate.
