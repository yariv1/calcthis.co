    California sales tax at a glance

    California’s base rate is 7.25% — 6% state plus 1.25% local — and cities and counties add voter-approved district taxes of 0.10% to 2.00%, so the rate you pay depends on where the sale happens. In the CDTFA’s October 1, 2026 table the combined rate runs from 7.25% to 11.75%. District taxes start on the first day of a calendar quarter.

    Los Angeles is 10.25%, San Francisco 8.625%, San Diego 7.75% and Sacramento 8.75%. Groceries and prescriptions are exempt, but clothing is not — so the calculator looks up your place and item type instead of guessing.

    What is taxed and what is exempt

    Item typeSales taxDetails
    Most other goods, including clothingTaxableMost goods are taxable, including clothing and shoes. Over-the-counter medicine is taxable too.
    Groceries (food for home)ExemptCold food and groceries bought to take home are exempt, including candy, juice and noncarbonated bottled water. Hot prepared food, carbonated drinks and alcohol are taxable — choose “Restaurant” or “Soda & alcohol”.
    Restaurant meals and hot prepared foodTaxableMeals and hot prepared food from restaurants and similar sellers are taxable, whether eaten there or taken away.
    Carbonated drinks and alcoholTaxableCarbonated beverages and alcoholic beverages sold in a store are taxable, even though other groceries are exempt.
    Prescription medicine and medical devicesExemptMedicine dispensed on a prescription and certain medical devices are exempt. Over-the-counter medicine is taxable.

    Shipping and delivery

    A delivery charge that is listed separately on the bill and covers the actual cost of carrier, mail or the seller’s own delivery after the sale is not taxed. A combined “shipping and handling” charge is taxed except for the actual shipping part — tick the box in the calculator to tax it in full. On a $100 item in Los Angeles with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $10.25; billed as one shipping-and-handling charge it is $11.28.

    Local taxes, special districts and fees

    • The rate depends on the place. California’s statewide rate is 7.25%. Cities and counties add district taxes of 0.10% to 2.00%, and a place can have more than one. In the October 1, 2026 table the combined rate runs from 7.25% to 11.75% (Lancaster and Palmdale). Search your city above.
    • District taxes can differ inside a city. The CDTFA says a ZIP code or mailing address does not always tell you the rate: district tax areas can cut through a city. The calculator uses the rate the CDTFA lists for the city or county, so for an exact address use the CDTFA’s find-a-rate-by-address tool.
    • What the 7.25% is made of. The state portion is 6% and the local portion is 1.25% (0.25% for county transportation funds and 1% for city or county operations). District taxes are added on top.
    • Groceries and drinks. Groceries, candy, juice and noncarbonated bottled water are exempt. Carbonated drinks and alcohol bought in a store are taxable, and so is hot prepared food and restaurant food, whether eaten there or taken away.
    • Clothing and medicine. Clothing has no exemption — it is taxed at your place’s rate. Medicine dispensed on a prescription and certain medical devices are exempt; over-the-counter medicine is taxable.
    • Delivery charges. Separately stated carrier or mail transport charges are not taxed. If shipping and handling are billed as one amount, only the actual shipping cost can be left out of the tax. If the item is sold at a delivered price, the whole price is taxed.
    • Cars, boats and aircraft. Vehicles are taxed at the rate of the place where they are registered, not where they are bought. The calculator does not apply that rule — pick the place where the vehicle will be registered.
    • Bought online or out of state. If the seller did not charge California tax you owe use tax at the combined rate for where you use the item. Pick your place and enter any tax the seller charged under Go advanced.
    • Rates change every quarter. District tax rates take effect on the first day of a calendar quarter (January, April, July or October). The rates here are from the October 1, 2026 table.

    Common questions about California sales tax

    What is the sales tax rate in California?

    California’s statewide sales tax rate is 7.25%, and most cities and counties add district taxes of 0.10% to 2.00% on top. In the CDTFA’s October 1, 2026 table the combined rate runs from 7.25% to 11.75% (Lancaster and Palmdale). Los Angeles is 10.25%, San Francisco 8.625%, San Diego 7.75%, San Jose 10% and Sacramento 8.75%. Search your city in the calculator above for your rate.

    What is the California sales tax made of?

    The 7.25% base is 6% state tax plus 1.25% local tax (0.25% for county transportation and 1% for city or county operations). Voter-approved district taxes are added on top, so a $100 purchase costs $107.75 in San Diego, $110.25 in Los Angeles and $111.00 in Long Beach. A place can have more than one district tax.

    Is food taxed in California?

    Groceries are exempt — a $100 grocery bill has $0.00 of tax, and candy, juice and noncarbonated bottled water count as groceries. Hot prepared food and restaurant meals are taxable whether eaten there or taken away: $100 of restaurant food in Los Angeles has $10.25 of tax. Carbonated drinks and alcohol bought in a store are taxable too ($10.25 on $100). Choose the item type to see the difference.

    Is clothing taxed in California?

    Yes. California has no clothing exemption, so clothing and shoes are taxed at your place’s rate — $10.25 on $100 in Los Angeles and $7.75 in San Diego.

    Are prescription drugs taxed in California?

    No. Medicine dispensed on a prescription and certain medical devices are exempt. Over-the-counter medicine is taxable. Choose “Prescription” as the item type to see the tax removed.

    Is shipping taxed in California?

    Not when the charge is listed separately on the bill and covers the actual cost of carrier, mail or the seller’s own delivery after the sale (CDTFA Regulation 1628). On a $100 item in Los Angeles with $10 shipping listed separately the tax is $10.25; billed as one shipping-and-handling charge it is $11.28, because only the actual shipping part can be left out. Tick the box in the calculator for the combined case.

    How do I take sales tax out of a total in California?

    Divide the total by 1 plus your combined rate. In Los Angeles (10.25%) a total of $110.25 is $100.00 before tax, with $10.25 of tax inside it. Switch the calculator to “Remove tax”, pick your city and enter the total.

    Do I owe California tax on things I buy online?

    Yes, when the seller does not charge California tax you owe use tax at the combined rate for where you use the item. A $3,000 item used in Los Angeles with no tax charged means $307.50 of use tax; if the seller charged 4% you still owe $187.50. Enter the tax the seller charged under Go advanced.

    Can I find my exact California rate from a ZIP code?

    Not reliably. The CDTFA says district tax areas can cut across a city, so a ZIP code or mailing address does not always give the right rate. This calculator uses the rate the CDTFA lists for your city or county, and the CDTFA’s find-a-rate-by-address tool gives the exact rate for one address. Palmdale, for example, is 11.75%.

    What sales tax do I pay on a car in California?

    Vehicles are taxed at the rate of the place where they will be registered, not where they are bought. Pick that place in the calculator — for a $30,000 car registered in Los Angeles the tax is $3,075.00. Registration fees and other charges are separate.
