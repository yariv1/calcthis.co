How to use the retatrutide dosage calculator

Choose Powder vial and type the peptide printed on the vial, then the water you add: the strength and the syringe units for the dose appear at once, on a syringe drawn to scale. Have a ready-made liquid instead? Choose Ready liquid and type the strength exactly as the label prints it.

Type the dose you were given. To see a whole plan, add steps: each is a dose and a number of weeks, with the injections a week. You get the units for every step, a timeline against your vials, the vials needed and what is left. Pick Water to add to work backwards from the units you want to draw.

The formulas

units = dose ÷ (vial ÷ water) × 100 STRENGTH = VIAL ÷ WATER · VOLUME = DOSE ÷ STRENGTH · UNITS = mL × 100

Worked out with this calculator: a 10 mg vial with 2 mL of water is 5 mg per mL. A 1 mg dose is 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 mL, which is 20 units. The vial holds 10 doses of 1 mg. With only 1 mL of water the strength doubles to 10 mg per mL and the same dose is 10 units. To draw 25 units for 1 mg, add 2.5 mL of water to the 10 mg vial.

Your own schedule: the arithmetic

Only to show how the steps add up (these are not suggested doses): at 5 mg per mL, 1 mg is 20 units and 2 mg is 40 units. 1 mg for 2 weeks and then 2 mg for 2 weeks at 1 injection a week is 4 injections and 6 mg, which fits in 1 vial of 10 mg with 4 mg left. When the rest of a vial cannot cover the next dose, a new vial is opened and the leftover is not used.

What the calculator does not do

It does not say what to take, how much or how often, and it does not read a label for you. Retatrutide is an investigational medicine, so there is no approved product label here to take a strength or a dose from. Use the numbers from your prescriber or pharmacy, and let a pharmacist check the strength, the water and the units. Titration ladders, “protocols” and half-life charts found on other pages state doses, so this page leaves them out.

Whole units and syringe size

If the draw is not a whole number of units, the page shows the closest whole unit and the dose it really gives. If a dose needs more than the syringe holds, it says so and shows how much water would make it fit.

Common questions

How do I calculate retatrutide syringe units?

Divide the peptide in the vial by the water you added to get the strength, divide your dose by the strength to get the volume, and multiply the volume in mL by 100 for units on a syringe with 100 units to the millilitre. For 10 mg in 2 mL (5 mg per mL), 1 mg is 0.2 mL, which is 20 units.

How much water should I add to the vial?

This page does not choose that for you. The water changes the strength but not the amount of peptide: 10 mg in 1 mL gives 10 units for 1 mg, in 2 mL 20 units. Use “Water to add” to find the water that gives the draw you want, or the table of other water volumes to compare.

Does this calculator tell me what dose to take?

No. It never suggests a dose, a schedule or a strength. You type the doses you were given by your prescriber or pharmacy, and it does the arithmetic.

Is retatrutide approved?

Eli Lilly describes retatrutide as investigational (press release of May 21, 2026). There is no approved product label to read a strength or a dose from, so check every number with a prescriber or pharmacist. This page is arithmetic, not medical advice.

How many vials does my schedule need?

Choose Powder vial, type the vial size and the water, then type each step as a dose and a number of weeks. The page opens a new vial when the rest cannot cover the next injection and lists the vials needed and what is left. For example (only to show the arithmetic): 1 mg for 2 weeks then 2 mg for 2 weeks at 1 a week uses 6 mg, so 1 vial of 10 mg with 4 mg left.

Why does it warn about whole units?

Syringes are marked in whole units, so a draw such as 33.33 units cannot be measured exactly. The warning shows the closest whole unit and the dose it really gives, so you can see the difference before you choose the water.

What is the difference between mg and mcg?

1 mg is 1,000 mcg. A mix-up is a thousandfold error, so the page shows every dose in both: 1 mg = 1,000 mcg.

What if I have a ready-made liquid and not a powder vial?

Choose “Ready liquid” and type the strength exactly as printed, for example 5 mg in 1 mL. There is no water step; the page gives the units for each dose and, if you type the liquid in the whole container, how many doses and containers your schedule needs.

Which syringe sizes does it handle?

A 1 mL, 0.5 mL or 0.3 mL syringe, all marked 100 units to the millilitre, so they hold 100, 50 and 30 units. If a dose does not fit the syringe you chose, the page says so and shows the water that would make it fit.
