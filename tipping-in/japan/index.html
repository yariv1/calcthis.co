Do you tip in Japan?

No. According to the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), tipping is generally not expected, and it is not common to tip for services such as those provided in bars, cafes, restaurants, taxis and hotels. There is no tip to calculate: pay the price on the bill.

The exceptions

A private guide or interpreter who is accustomed to overseas practices may accept a tip as a gesture of gratitude, though tipping is still not expected. JNTO describes a custom called kokorozuke (“coming from the heart”) that applies in some situations.

How to give a tip

If you do choose to tip, JNTO says it is customary to put it in an envelope and give it discreetly. Small envelopes are sold at convenience stores, stationery stores and 100 yen stores.
