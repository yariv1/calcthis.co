How to use the body recomposition calculator

Type your weight, height, age and activity level, then choose your focus: lose more fat, lose fat and build muscle, or build more muscle. All three are always shown side by side, so you can compare without running it again.

Know your body fat? Type it and the calories use your lean mass (Katch-McArdle). Don’t? Press Estimate it from my measurements and the page works it out from your waist, neck and (for women) hip. Open Go advanced to type the calories you burn if you know them, set a target body fat to see the fat to lose, and choose your meals a day.

What body recomposition means

Body recomposition is losing fat and keeping or building muscle at the same time, so your shape changes more than your weight. The research on it is thin, which is why this page shows the one hard number I could read: a 2022 meta-analysis found that a deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented gains in lean mass, and the ruler marks that line.

Why the scale barely moves

A small cut is a small number on the scale (0.18 kg a week at 200 kcal under, on the simple rule) and any muscle gained hides part of it. Measure your waist, take photos and track the weights you lift as well.

Sources

  • Murphy C, Koehler K, Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: a meta-analysis and meta-regression, Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports 2022;32(1):125–137 (abstract read via Europe PMC, 2026-11-07): an energy deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented gains in lean mass; lean-mass gains were impaired in the deficit group compared with controls while strength gains were comparable; people who lift to preserve lean mass during weight loss should avoid deficits larger than 500 kcal a day. This is the “~500 kcal line” on the ruler.
  • Ribeiro AS et al., Effects of different dietary energy intake following resistance training on muscle mass and body fat in bodybuilders: a pilot study, Journal of Human Kinetics 2019;70:125–134 (abstract read via Europe PMC): 11 male bodybuilders over 4 weeks; the group eating more (about 67.5 kcal per kg a day against about 50.1) gained more body fat (+7.4% against +0.8%) and more muscle (+2.7% against +1.1%). A pilot in 11 men, so it is not used to set any number here.
  • Protein 1.6–2.2 g per kg a day, fat 0.5–1.5 g per kg and carbohydrate of at least 3 g per kg to fuel training: Iraki J et al., Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season, Sports 2019;7(7):154 (abstract read). That review is about gaining, not cutting; I found no source I could read for protein in a deficit, so this calculator uses 2 g per kg for all three focuses, inside that range, and the same 1 g per kg of fat.

Common questions

What is body recomposition?

Body recomposition means losing fat and keeping or building muscle at the same time, so your shape changes more than your weight. It needs strength training, enough protein and a calorie intake near what you burn rather than a large cut.

How many calories should I eat for body recomposition?

Start from the calories you burn and eat a little under them, or exactly that. For a woman of 70 kg and 168 cm, 35 years old, with moderate activity, you burn about 2,192 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor 1,414 × 1.55). This calculator’s three presets are 1,792 kcal (400 under, more fat loss), 1,992 kcal (200 under, both) and 2,192 kcal (what you burn, more muscle focus). They are presets of this page, not from a study.

Can you lose fat and build muscle at the same time?

The 2022 meta-analysis I read found that an energy deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented gains in lean mass, while strength gains stayed comparable. That is why the presets here stay under 500 kcal under what you burn, and why the ruler marks that line. How much muscle a given person gains while losing fat I did not find a source for, so the calculator gives no muscle-gain projection.

How big a calorie deficit is too big for recomposition?

In the 2022 meta-analysis, a deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented gains in lean mass, and the authors say people who lift to preserve lean mass during weight loss should avoid deficits larger than 500 kcal a day. The calculator’s biggest preset is 400 kcal under what you burn.

How much protein for body recomposition?

This calculator uses 2 g per kg of body weight a day, inside the 1.6–2.2 g per kg range from a 2019 review of off-season nutrition. For 70 kg that is 140 g a day, the same every day, split across your meals. That review is about gaining; I found no readable source specific to cutting, so I did not push the number higher.

Why does the scale barely move on body recomposition?

On the simple rule of 7,700 kcal per kg, a 200 kcal cut is about 0.18 kg a week of fat and no cut is none, and any muscle you gain hides part of that. So watch your waist, photos and the weights you lift as well as the scale.

Do I need to know my body fat?

No. Without it, calories you burn come from your age, height, weight and activity (Mifflin-St Jeor). If you give a body fat percentage, the calculator uses Katch-McArdle, which is based on lean mass. You can also estimate it from your waist and neck (and hip for women) with the US Navy method, right on the page.

How long will it take to reach my target body fat?

Type your current and target body fat under Go advanced. The calculator keeps your lean mass the same and works out the fat to lose. For example 70 kg at 30% body fat and a target of 25% means about 4.7 kg of fat to lose, about 13 weeks at 400 kcal under what you burn or 26 weeks at 200 kcal under, on the simple rule. Building muscle would change the result.

How many days a week should I lift?

Most recomposition guides suggest at least 3 strength sessions a week. The studies I read give no number, so the calculator shows it as a reminder under Go advanced and does not change the calories with it.

Is body recomposition safe for everyone?

This page is for information only. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, take a GLP-1 or other weight medicine, have kidney disease or a history of disordered eating, ask a clinician; the calculator gives no numbers for those cases.
