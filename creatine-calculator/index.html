How to use the creatine calculator

Type your body weight and choose how to start: a loading week (a higher dose for 5–7 days, then the daily dose), daily dose only (the daily dose from day 1, slower) or already taking it. You get the dose, the schedule, the two routes side by side and a chart of how muscle stores fill.

Open Go advanced to change the loading length and doses a day, type your own maintenance dose, pick the date you want full stores by, or enter your tub size, price and scoop to see how long it lasts and what it costs.

The numbers

loading = 0.3 g × body weight (kg) a day for 5–7 days, to the whole gram MAINTENANCE: 3–5 G A DAY ONCE STORES ARE FULL (ISSN)

80 kg → 24 g a day, four doses of 6 g, then 3 g a day. In the first 28 days that is 231 g with a 7-day load or 84 g without.

How much creatine should I take?

The ISSN position stand describes loading (about 0.3 g per kg a day, as four doses, for 5–7 days) and maintenance (3–5 g a day, 5–10 g for larger athletes). Taking 3 g a day for 28 days reaches full stores more slowly.

Creatine and water

Creatine holds a little water in the muscle. The position stand reports about 0.5–1.0 L of short-term fluid retention during loading, in proportion to the weight gained. It gives no extra water target, so neither does this page; for a general daily amount see the water intake calculator.

Pair it with protein and training

Creatine works with training. For a daily protein target use the protein intake calculator, and for the full split the macro calculator.

Sources

  • Kreider RB, Kalman DS, Antonio J, et al., International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine, J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017;14:18 (full text read via Europe PMC, 2026-11-07): a normal diet with 1–2 g a day of creatine leaves muscle creatine about 60–80% saturated; the most effective way to raise stores is 5 g of creatine monohydrate (approximately 0.3 g/kg body weight) four times a day for 5–7 days; once stores are full they are maintained with 3–5 g a day, although larger athletes may need 5–10 g a day; an alternative is 3 g a day for 28 days, a gradual increase; stores take 4–6 weeks to return to baseline after stopping; loading can retain about 0.5–1.0 L of fluid, in proportion to the weight gained; up to 30 g a day for 5 years was safe and well tolerated in healthy people; carbohydrate or carbohydrate with protein promoted more consistent creatine retention.
  • The 0.03 g per kg a day maintenance rule of thumb is used by other creatine calculators; it is not in the position stand text I read. This calculator applies it only as a starting point, held between 3 and 5 g, and lets you type your own dose.
  • The ISSN text prints the loading dose as 5 g (approximately 0.3 g/kg) four times a day; four doses of 5 g make 20 g a day, about 0.3 g per kg a day for a 70 kg person, so this page uses 0.3 g per kg per day like other creatine calculators.
  • The chart is a schematic: it starts at 70%, the middle of the 60–80% diet range, and rises in a straight line to full at day 5–7 (loading) or day 28 (3 g a day). Real curves differ between people.

Common questions

How much creatine should I take per day?

The ISSN position stand says stores are maintained with 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate a day once they are full, and larger athletes may need 5–10 g. For 80 kg this calculator gives 3 g a day.

How much creatine per kg of body weight?

Loading uses about 0.3 g per kg a day for 5–7 days: 24 g a day at 80 kg, split into four doses of 6 g. For maintenance many calculators use 0.03 g per kg; the calculator holds that between 3 and 5 g, so 80 kg gets 3 g, and you can type your own dose under Go advanced.

Do I need to load creatine?

No. Loading fills muscle stores in 5–7 days. Taking 3 g a day from day 1 raises them gradually over about 28 days, with less effect on training until they are full. The calculator shows both side by side: 231 g in the first 28 days with a 7-day load, 84 g without.

How long does creatine take to work?

About 5–7 days with a loading phase and about 28 days at 3 g a day, according to the ISSN position stand. If you need to be full by a date, enter it under Go advanced to see when to start.

How much water should I drink on creatine?

The position stand reports that loading can retain about 0.5–1.0 L of fluid, in proportion to the weight gained. The text I read gives no extra water target, so this page does not invent one; for a general daily target use the water intake calculator.

Does creatine cause water weight?

Yes, a little at first: the ISSN position stand reports short-term fluid retention of about 0.5–1.0 L during loading, in proportion to the weight gained. That is water in the muscle, not fat.

Is creatine safe?

In the position stand, up to 30 g a day for 5 years was safe and well tolerated in healthy people, and collegiate football players taking 5–10 g a day for 21 months showed no clinically significant change in kidney markers. People with kidney disease, who are pregnant or breastfeeding, or who take medication should ask a clinician; this page gives no dose for them.

When should I take creatine?

The position stand reports that taking creatine with carbohydrate, or carbohydrate and protein, promoted more consistent retention. I found no read source for a best time of day, so take it when you will remember.

What happens when I stop taking creatine?

Muscle creatine takes 4–6 weeks to return to baseline, and the position stand found no evidence that your own creatine production falls below baseline afterwards.
