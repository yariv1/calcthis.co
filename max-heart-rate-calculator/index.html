How to use the max heart rate calculator

Pick female or male, enter your age and, if you take beta blockers, switch that to Yes. You get the six formulas side by side with the spread between them; click one to make it the headline, or choose it under Headline formula.

Open Go advanced and type a max heart rate you measured to see how far it is from the headline and to base the percent table on your own number.

Max heart rate formulas

Tanaka: 208 − 0.7 × age · Fox: 220 − age · Nes (HUNT): 211 − 0.64 × age GULATI (WOMEN): 206 − 0.88 × AGE · INBAR: 205.8 − 0.685 × AGE · OAKLAND: 192 − 0.007 × AGE²

At 35: Tanaka 184, Fox 185, Nes 189, Gulati 175, Inbar 182, Oakland 183 bpm — 14 beats between the highest and the lowest.

Find your real max: the 4×4 test

  1. Warm up thoroughly, until you sweat.
  2. Do two intervals of 4 minutes, hard enough that you are too short of breath to talk, with 3 minutes of active rest between them.
  3. Start a third interval and, two minutes in, increase your speed further and keep going until you are too exhausted to continue.
  4. Your HRmax is the highest heart rate you reach. No monitor? Hold two fingers to your neck for 30 seconds right after.

This is NTNU’s description. Check with a clinician first if you have a heart condition, take heart medicines or are not sure a maximal test is safe for you.

Beta blockers

NTNU explains that beta blockers block adrenaline and so reduce maximum heart rate, by an amount that depends on the dose. The formulas on this page come from healthy adults and do not apply to you if you take them — choose Yes above and ask your clinician how to exercise.

Why the formulas disagree

Each comes from a different group of people. Even the best of them is an average: in the HUNT study of 3,320 healthy adults the standard error of an age formula was 10.8 bpm, with no difference by gender, activity level, fitness or BMI. For the zones built on this number, see the heart rate zone calculator.

Sources

  • Tanaka H, Monahan KD, Seals DR, Age-predicted maximal heart rate revisited, J Am Coll Cardiol 2001;37:153–156 (abstract read via Europe PMC, 2026-11-06): a meta-analysis of 351 studies and 18,712 subjects gave HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age; a laboratory study of 514 healthy subjects gave 209 − 0.7 × age; the line did not differ between men and women or with habitual activity.
  • Gulati M et al., Heart rate response to exercise stress testing in asymptomatic women: the St. James Women Take Heart Project, Circulation 2010;122:130–137 (abstract read via Europe PMC): in 5,437 asymptomatic women, mean peak heart rate = 206 − 0.88 × age.
  • Nes BM et al., Age-predicted maximal heart rate in healthy subjects: the HUNT Fitness Study, Scand J Med Sci Sports 2013;23:697–704 (abstract read via Europe PMC): in 3,320 healthy men and women, HRmax = 211 − 0.64 × age, standard error of the estimate 10.8 bpm, with no interaction with gender, physical activity, VO2max or BMI.
  • NTNU CERG, Maximum Heart Rate Calculator (read 2026-11-06): beta blockers reduce maximum heart rate; the 4×4 interval test for finding your real HRmax; 220 − age can underestimate HRmax by up to 40 beats per minute in seniors.
  • Fox / Haskell (220 − age), Inbar (205.8 − 0.685 × age) and Oakland nonlinear (192 − 0.007 × age²): the constants are as published by Omnicalculator (opened 2026-11-06); the original papers were not read.

Common questions

How do I calculate my maximum heart rate?

The best known rule is 220 − age (Fox / Haskell): at 40 that is 180 bpm. The Tanaka formula, 208 − 0.7 × age, gives 180 at 40 and the Nes (HUNT) formula, 211 − 0.64 × age, gives 185. The calculator shows all six at once and lets you pick the headline.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It is a rough average. The Tanaka study found the equation had never been validated in a sample with enough older adults, and the NTNU HUNT team says it can underestimate HRmax by up to 40 beats per minute in seniors. At 35 the six formulas here differ by 14 bpm, and the HUNT study put the standard error of an age formula at 10.8 bpm for any one person.

Which max heart rate formula is best?

Tanaka (208 − 0.7 × age) comes from a meta-analysis of 351 studies and was confirmed in 514 healthy adults, and Nes (211 − 0.64 × age) from 3,320 healthy adults; both found no difference between men and women. The calculator defaults to Tanaka. None of them replaces a measured value.

What is the max heart rate for women?

Tanaka and Nes both found no difference between men and women. The Gulati formula, 206 − 0.88 × age, was derived from 5,437 asymptomatic women: at 35 it gives 175 bpm against 184 for Tanaka. Pick Female to see it marked as the women-specific row.

How do I find my real max heart rate?

Use a maximal test. NTNU describes a 4×4 interval test: warm up until you sweat; do two 4-minute intervals hard enough that you are too short of breath to talk, with 3 minutes of active rest between; start a third interval and, two minutes in, push harder until you cannot continue. Your HRmax is the highest heart rate you reach. Without a monitor, hold two fingers to your neck for 30 seconds right after. Enter the number under Go advanced to compare it with the formulas.

Do beta blockers change my max heart rate?

Yes. NTNU explains that beta blockers block adrenaline and so reduce maximum heart rate, by an amount that depends on the dose. The formulas come from healthy adults, so if you take beta blockers choose Yes — the calculator warns that its numbers are too high — and ask your clinician how to exercise.

What heart rate is 70% or 85% of my max?

Multiply your max by the percent. The table under the result does it for 50% to 100% from your chosen formula, or from your measured max when you enter one. For named training zones, including Zone 2, use the heart rate zone calculator.

Can my heart rate go above the formula’s number?

Yes. The formulas are averages for a group; in the HUNT study the standard error was 10.8 bpm, so many healthy people sit more than 10 bpm above or below. That is why the calculator shows a ±10.8 bpm band and lets you add your measured max.

What is the difference between max heart rate and zones?

Max heart rate is one number: the highest rate your heart reaches. Zones divide the range below it, often as percents of max or with your resting heart rate. The Zone 2 calculator and the heart rate zone calculator take it from here.
