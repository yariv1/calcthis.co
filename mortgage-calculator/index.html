How to use the mortgage calculator

Enter the home price, your down payment (as a percentage, or switch the box to dollars), the interest rate and the loan term in years — or tap 10, 15, 20 or 30 years. The result updates as you type: your monthly payment, a chart of how the balance falls, how much of the loan is principal and how much is interest, and a full amortization schedule. Type an amount in Extra payment per month to see how much interest it saves and how much sooner the loan ends.

This calculator covers fixed-rate loans. The payment is principal and interest only until you add property tax, insurance, PMI or HOA fees under Go advanced, which then show as a line-by-line total.

The mortgage payment formula

M = P · r(1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1) P = LOAN AMOUNT · r = MONTHLY RATE · n = NUMBER OF PAYMENTS

For a $320,000 loan (a $400,000 home with 20% down) at 6.5% over 30 years: r = 6.5% ÷ 12 = 0.0054167 and n = 360, so (1 + r)360 = 6.9918. Then M = $320,000 × 0.0054167 × 6.9918 ÷ (6.9918 − 1) = $2,022.62 a month. Over 360 payments you pay $408,142 in interest on top of the $320,000 you borrowed.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Interest is charged on what you still owe, and you owe the most at the start. On that loan the first payment is $1,733.33 in interest and only $289.28 in principal. Each payment shrinks the balance a little, so the interest share falls and the principal share rises. Here principal first outweighs interest in payment 233, in year 20. The calculator marks that month on your balance chart and shows each year’s split in the strip below it.

What extra payments do

Extra money goes straight to principal, so the interest it would have earned the lender is never charged. Adding $200 a month to the loan above ends it after 281 payments — 6 years 7 months sooner — and saves $105,429 in interest. Go advanced adds a yearly extra payment, a one-time lump sum, and the bi-weekly option (half a payment every two weeks, which is one extra monthly payment a year).

PMI and the 20% down payment

Lenders usually add private mortgage insurance when you put down less than 20%. It is typically charged until your balance falls to 80% of the home price. On a $400,000 home with 5% down — a $380,000 loan at 6.5% — the balance falls to $320,000 after payment 124, in year 11, so PMI stops from payment 125. At 0.8% of the loan a year, PMI would be $253.33 a month until then. Enter the PMI rate under Go advanced and the calculator finds the exact month it ends.

Taxes, insurance and HOA fees

Your real monthly cost is more than principal and interest. Property tax is a yearly percentage of the home’s value that varies a lot by location; insurance and HOA fees are fixed amounts you can find on your quote. Under Go advanced, a 1.2% property tax ($4,800 a year, $400 a month) and $1,500 of insurance ($125 a month) take the loan above to $2,547.62 a month. You can also set how fast these costs rise each year.

15-year or 30-year?

A shorter term costs more each month but far less in total. On $320,000 at 6.5%, 30 years is $2,022.62 a month with $408,142 of interest; 15 years is $2,787.54 a month with $181,758. The comparison table in every result shows 10, 15, 20 and 30 years for your own numbers, so you can see the trade-off at a glance.

More with Go advanced

Go advanced adds the loan start date (for the payoff date and schedule dates), property tax as a percentage or a dollar amount, home insurance, PMI with its automatic end date, HOA fees and other costs with a yearly increase, a once-a-year extra payment, a one-time extra payment, the bi-weekly comparison, Canadian semi-annual compounding, and a copy button.

Common questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

The principal-and-interest payment is M = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (the yearly rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. For a $320,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years, r = 0.0054167, n = 360 and the payment is $2,022.62. Property tax, insurance, PMI and HOA fees are added on top.

Why is so much of my early payment interest?

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe, and the balance is highest at the start. On the $320,000 loan at 6.5%, the first payment of $2,022.62 is $1,733.33 interest and only $289.28 principal. Because each payment lowers the balance, the interest share shrinks and the principal share grows — here principal first outweighs interest in payment 233, in year 20.

How much do extra payments save?

On the same loan, adding $200 a month cuts the loan from 360 payments to 281 — paid off 6 years 7 months sooner — and saves $105,429 in interest. Every extra dollar goes straight to principal, so it stops accruing interest for the rest of the loan. Enter an amount in the extra payment box to see your own savings and the faster balance curve.

What is PMI and when does it end?

Private mortgage insurance is a monthly charge lenders add when you put down less than 20%. It is usually charged until your balance falls to 80% of the home price. On a $400,000 home with 5% down (a $380,000 loan at 6.5%), the balance falls to $320,000 after payment 124 (year 11), so PMI stops from payment 125. If PMI costs 0.8% of the loan a year, that is $253.33 a month until then. Add PMI under Go advanced to see the exact date.

Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better?

A 15-year loan has a higher payment but costs far less interest. On $320,000 at 6.5%, the 30-year payment is $2,022.62 with $408,142 of total interest; the 15-year payment is $2,787.54 with $181,758. The comparison table under every result shows 10, 15, 20 and 30 years side by side for your own numbers.
