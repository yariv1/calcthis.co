How to use the GMI calculator

Enter the mean glucose from your CGM report in mg/dL or mmol/L — or a GMI to work backwards — and the other fields fill in. Add your latest lab A1C to see the gap between the two. The scale marks your GMI (and your lab A1C) against the normal, prediabetes and diabetes ranges.

Below the result the calculator shows the ADAG-formula A1C for the same average, so you can see why a general A1C calculator and your CGM app can disagree. Under Go advanced you can paste a list of readings and let it average them.

The GMI formula

GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean glucose (mg/dL) GLUCOSE MANAGEMENT INDICATOR

A mean glucose of 154 mg/dL: 3.31 + 0.02392 × 154 = 7.0%. A mean of 8.6 mmol/L is 155 mg/dL, a GMI of 7.0%. Backwards: (GMI − 3.31) ÷ 0.02392, so 7.0% is 154 mg/dL.

GMI vs A1C

An A1C test measures glycated hemoglobin, which builds up over about three months. GMI is worked out from the average glucose your sensor recorded. In the research that defined GMI, about 28% of people had a lab A1C and GMI that differed by 0.5 percentage points or more, so a gap is common and not an error.

Common questions

What is GMI?

GMI, the Glucose Management Indicator, is an estimate of what your A1C would be, worked out from the average glucose of a continuous glucose monitor (CGM). It replaced the older term “estimated A1C” in 2018 so people would stop expecting it to match a lab result exactly.

How do I calculate GMI?

GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean glucose (mg/dL). A mean glucose of 154 mg/dL gives 3.31 + 0.02392 × 154 = 7.0%. If your report is in mmol/L, multiply by 18.016 first — 8.6 mmol/L is 155 mg/dL, a GMI of 7.0%.

How do I convert GMI to average glucose?

Reverse the formula: mean glucose (mg/dL) = (GMI − 3.31) ÷ 0.02392. A GMI of 7.0% is 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L). Type the GMI into the calculator and the glucose fills in.

Why is my GMI different from my lab A1C?

They measure different things: A1C reflects glycated hemoglobin over about three months, GMI reflects the glucose your sensor recorded in the period you chose. In the research that defined GMI, about 28% of people had a lab A1C and GMI that differed by 0.5 percentage points or more, so a gap is common. Add your lab A1C in the calculator to see yours; raise a large gap with your care team.

Is GMI the same as the A1C formula on an A1C calculator?

No. An A1C calculator uses the ADAG formula (A1C = (average glucose + 46.7) ÷ 28.7). GMI uses a formula fitted to CGM data. They give the same answer near 154 mg/dL and drift apart from there: at 250 mg/dL the ADAG formula gives 10.3% and GMI 9.3%. The calculator shows both side by side.

Can GMI diagnose diabetes?

No. GMI is an estimate for people who already use a CGM, and a diagnosis needs a laboratory test, repeated if abnormal. Use it to talk with your care team, not to change treatment on your own.

Can I enter a list of readings?

Yes. Open Go advanced, choose mg/dL or mmol/L and paste your readings, one per line or separated by commas; the calculator averages them and fills in the GMI. A CGM report’s mean glucose is more representative than a handful of readings.
