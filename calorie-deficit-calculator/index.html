How to use the calorie deficit calculator

Choose your units and sex, then either let the calculator estimate your maintenance calories from your age, height, weight and activity level, or choose I know it and type your own number. Add your current weight and, if you want a timeline, a goal weight.

Then set the deficit the way that suits you: Deficit (kcal a day), Pace (kg or lb a week, or a percentage of your weight), Date (the deficit you need to reach your goal by a day) or I eat (type what you eat and see the deficit it gives). Use the exercise chips to see how burning more changes what you can eat, and tap a row of the side-by-side table to use it.

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit is eating fewer calories than your body burns. Your body makes up the difference from stored energy, mostly body fat. The size of the deficit decides how fast you lose: a bigger gap loses faster, but is harder to keep up and can cut into the food you need.

The calorie deficit formula

Deficit = calories you burn − calories you eat 7,700 KCAL ≈ 1 KG OF BODY FAT (3,500 KCAL ≈ 1 LB)

Take the example: a 35-year-old woman, 165 cm and 80 kg, moderately active. Her BMR is 1,495 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor), and 1,495 × 1.55 gives a maintenance of 2,318 kcal a day. Eating 1,818 kcal is a deficit of 500 kcal (22% of maintenance), which loses about 0.45 kg (1 lb) a week.

Simple rule or realistic?

The simple rule divides the energy in the weight you want to lose (7,700 kcal per kg) by your deficit. It treats your body as a machine that burns the same every day. In real life you burn about 16 kcal less per kg lost, so the same intake becomes a smaller deficit as you get lighter. The realistic estimate allows for that, so it is a little longer — and a very small deficit can level off before you reach the goal. The weight curve plots both.

Safe limits

  • A floor on what you eat. The calculator flags eating under 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men — the minimums usually advised without medical supervision.
  • A cap on the pace. It flags a plan that loses more than 1% of your body weight a week.
  • Under 18, pregnant or with a medical condition: talk to a doctor before cutting calories.

Common questions

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit means eating fewer calories than your body burns in a day, so it makes up the gap from stored energy, mostly fat. For the woman in the example (2,318 kcal maintenance), eating 1,818 kcal is a deficit of 500 kcal a day, about 0.45 kg (1 lb) a week.

How do I calculate my calorie deficit?

Work out your maintenance calories (what you burn in a day), then subtract what you eat. Maintenance is your BMR times an activity factor: for the example, 1,495 × 1.55 = 2,318 kcal. Eat 1,818 and the deficit is 2,318 − 1,818 = 500 kcal. Or start from the other end: a loss of 1 lb a week needs about 500 kcal a day, because a pound of body fat is roughly 3,500 kcal.

How big should my calorie deficit be?

Most plans use 250 to 750 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.25 to 0.75 kg (0.5 to 1.5 lb) a week at the example's weight. A deficit of 500 kcal is 22% of maintenance. Bigger deficits lose faster but are harder to keep up, and the calculator flags any plan below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, or one that loses more than 1% of your body weight a week.

How long will it take to lose weight in a calorie deficit?

With the simple rule (7,700 kcal per kg), losing 10 kg at a 500 kcal deficit takes about 22 weeks. A more realistic estimate is 26 weeks, because you burn less as you get lighter — the calculator shows both and plots them on one chart.

Why is the realistic time longer than the simple rule?

The simple rule assumes you burn the same number of calories every day. In reality maintenance falls by about 16 kcal for every kg you lose, so a fixed intake becomes a smaller deficit over time. That is why weight loss slows down and why you may need to eat a little less, or move a little more, as you go.

Should I cut calories or exercise more?

Either works, and many people do both. A deficit of 500 kcal could be 500 kcal less food, or 300 kcal less food plus 200 kcal burned by exercise. The calculator lets you add exercise and shows how much it raises what you can eat, with the safe minimum marked on the bar.

Is a 1,000 calorie deficit safe?

For many people it is too aggressive: at the example's maintenance of 2,318 kcal it would mean eating 1,318 kcal, close to the 1,200 kcal a day minimum usually advised without medical supervision. It also loses about 0.91 kg a week. Larger deficits are best discussed with a doctor or dietitian.

What if I already know my maintenance calories?

Choose “I know it” under Maintenance and type the number — for example from tracking your intake and weight for a few weeks. The calculator then skips the estimate and uses your number as given. It still needs your weight, and a goal weight if you want a timeline.
